## 1. What do the meta-analyses say about grammar instruction, sentence combining and explicit instruction?

### Takeaway
Across the major meta-analyses, traditional grammar instruction is the one practice that does not help (null to negative effects: -0.32 in Writing Next for grades 4-12; negative in Koster et al. 2015 for grades 4-6; non-significant, about -0.41, in Graham et al. 2012 for elementary), while sentence combining is reliably positive (0.50 in Writing Next) and explicit strategy instruction and goal setting are among the largest effects (0.82 to 2.03).

### Cited Findings
- **Graham & Perin (2007)**, "A Meta-Analysis of Writing Instruction for Adolescent Students," *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 99, 445-476; also published as *Writing Next* (Alliance for Excellent Education / Carnegie Corporation). Among 11 interventions, **sentence combining ES = 0.50 (5 studies)** and **grammar instruction ES = -0.32**, the only negative value among the 11. Strategy instruction 0.82, summarization 0.82, peer assistance 0.75. — [Oregon Dept. of Education Writing Next synopsis](https://www.oregon.gov/ode/educator-resources/essentialskills/Documents/essentialskillwriting_hs_level2_writing_next_synopsis.pdf); [Graham & Perin 2007 PDF (pbworks mirror)](https://bridgestolearning2009.pbworks.com/f/graham&perin07.pdf); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228981551_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Writing_Instruction_for_Adolescent_Students)
- Writing Next: the sentence-combining intervention "taught students to improve the quality of their writing by crafting more complex sentences," combining two simple sentences into one. — [Oregon synopsis](https://www.oregon.gov/ode/educator-resources/essentialskills/Documents/essentialskillwriting_hs_level2_writing_next_synopsis.pdf)
- Graham & Perin 2007: the control conditions in the sentence-combining studies were "relatively homogeneous, primarily involving grammar instruction" (so the 0.50 is sentence combining versus traditional grammar, not versus nothing). — [search summary of Graham & Perin 2007](https://bridgestolearning2009.pbworks.com/f/graham&perin07.pdf)
- Writing Next: when split by writer ability, the sentence-combining effect for weaker writers was 0.46, but only one study (Saddler & Graham 2005) examined low-achieving writers. — [Saddler & Graham 2005 ERIC record and related summaries](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ688034) (secondary summary)
- A UCL paper (Wyse et al., *Journal of Writing Research*, 2025) cites Graham & Perin as showing grammar teaching had a statistically significant negative effect on pupils' writing. — [Wyse et al. 2025 JoWR (UCL Discovery)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10219086/1/Wyse%20et%20al%20JoWR%202025.pdf) (search summary only)
- **Graham, McKeown, Kiuhara & Harris (2012)**, "A Meta-Analysis of Writing Instruction for Students in the Elementary Grades," *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 104(4), 879-896. 115 documents; an intervention needed 4 studies to be included. All interventions but one produced a statistically significant effect; the exception was **grammar instruction**. A secondary review table gives grammar **ES = -0.41 (4 studies), 95% CI [-1.2, 0.38]** (spans zero). Other effects: strategy instruction 1.02, transcription skills 0.55, assessing writing 0.42, comprehensive writing programs 0.42, process approach 0.40 (16 studies), extra writing time 0.30. — [ASU Elsevier Pure record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-writing-instruction-for-students-in-the-elemen/); [ACU Research Bank record](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8v0z1/a-meta-analysis-of-writing-instruction-for-students-in-the-elementary-grades); [Texas Center for Learning Disabilities summary](https://texasldcenter.org/?p=311). Conflict: the -0.41 figure appears in a secondary review; the abstract only says grammar was the one non-significant intervention. Verify the exact value in the original.
- **Koster, Tribushinina, de Jong & van den Bergh (2015)**, "Teaching children to write: A meta-analysis of writing intervention research," *Journal of Writing Research*, 7(2), 249-274, DOI 10.17239/jowr-2015.07.02.2. Grades 4-6, regular classrooms, ten intervention categories. **Goal setting ES = 2.03, strategy instruction 0.96, feedback 0.88, text structure instruction 0.76, peer assistance 0.59.** Grammar instruction (and the process approach) yielded **negative average effects**; exact grammar ES not shown in search results. A critique notes only four grammar studies with elementary pupils. — [JoWR article page](https://www.jowr.org/index.php/jowr/article/view/661); [UvA-DARE full text PDF](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/2575422/172086_JoWR_2015_vol7_nr2_Koster_et_al.pdf); [UvA DARE record](https://dare.uva.nl/search?identifier=da520336-bc44-46b1-8557-d0acca95af7a)
- Visible Learning (Hattie) lists sentence-combining programs at a weighted mean ES of only 0.12, from two older meta-analyses; far below Graham & Perin's 0.50, likely because of different comparisons and older studies. — [Visible Learning MetaX: sentence-combining programs](https://www.visiblelearningmetax.com/influences/view/sentence-combining_programs)
- Neville & Searls (1991), meta-analytic review of 24 studies: sentence combining had a larger effect on **reading comprehension** at the elementary level than at high school or college. — [Reading Research and Instruction, 31(1)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19388079109558072)

### Inferences
- The consistent pattern is: grammar taught as naming/parsing, separate from writing, does not transfer to composition; grammar practiced as sentence building (combining, constructing) does. For a curriculum built on explicit grammar symbols and sentence formulas, the evidence supports the "explicit" part only when the symbols are used to build and revise real sentences, not as labeling drills.
- The largest effects in every meta-analysis belong to explicit, teacher-modeled instruction (strategy instruction, goal setting, text structure). This supports the curriculum's premise of "explicit first," but the explicit content that works best is procedures and structures for composing, not grammatical terminology alone.
- Because the sentence-combining comparison condition was usually traditional grammar, the 0.50 is effectively a head-to-head win for sentence-level practice over grammar drills.

### Gaps
- Graham, Harris & Santangelo (2015, *Elementary School Journal*) and Graham et al. (2016, WWC-related or "Evidence-based writing practices") were not found in any search result in this session: **unverified**.
- Exact Koster et al. (2015) grammar effect size and Graham et al. (2012) grammar confidence interval not confirmed from full text (jowr.org, pure.uva.nl not reachable).
- The ScienceDirect meta-analysis of ~40 years of written-expression interventions for students with learning disabilities (c. 2024, *Educational Research Review*) includes a sentence-combining category, but its effect size was not in the snippet. — [ScienceDirect record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X24000010)

## 2. Evidence that traditional, decontextualized grammar does not improve writing, and what distinguishes effective approaches

### Takeaway
The EPPI-Centre reviews (Andrews et al. 2006) found little evidence that formal grammar or syntax teaching improves the accuracy or quality of 5-16-year-olds' writing, while sentence combining had a more positive effect; the dividing line is whether grammar is practiced as building sentences in writing versus naming parts in isolation.

### Cited Findings
- **Andrews, Torgerson, Beverton, Freeman, Locke, Low, Robinson & Zhu (2006)**, "The effect of grammar teaching on writing development," *British Educational Research Journal*, 32(1), 39-55. Reports two systematic reviews on teaching grammar to improve the quality and accuracy of writing of 5-16-year-olds in English. Little evidence that teaching formal grammar is effective; teaching of syntax appeared to have no influence on accuracy or writing quality; sentence combining (including embedding) had a more positive effect; but research quality was insufficient to prove the case for either approach. The review screened 4,691 papers from 1900 onward (Canada, US, UK, Australia, New Zealand). — [Wiley/BERJ abstract](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01411920500401997); [ERIC EJ729028](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ729028); [York Research Database](https://pure.york.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/the-effect-of-grammar-teaching-on-writing-development); [EPPI-Centre review, Dec 2004](http://eppi.ioe.ac.uk/cms/Portals/0/PDF%20reviews%20and%20summaries/eng_rv7.pdf?ver=2006-03-02-124853-077)
- Debate: Hudson argued the EPPI review wrongly treated sentence combining as not being grammar teaching; Andrews responded that sentence combining is itself a form of grammar teaching. — [Andrews 2010 chapter "Teaching sentence-level grammar for writing: the evidence so far"](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10000952/1/Andrews2010Teaching(chapter).pdf); [Hudson, "Research on grammar for writing"](https://dickhudson.com/research-on-grammar-for-writing/)
- A secondary source cited sentence-combining effect sizes of 0.85-1.31 attributed to the EPPI work; could not be confirmed as coming from the review. — [search summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/44924255_The_effect_of_grammar_teaching_on_writing_development) (**unverified**)
- **Englicious RCT (Wyse, Aarts, Anders, De Gennaro, Dockrell, Manyukhina, Sing, Torgerson, et al.; Nuffield report 2022; JoWR 2025)**: 70 Year 2 classes in England (pupils aged 6-7; 1,736 recruited, about 1,246 analysed). No effect of the grammar teaching intervention on narrative writing; overall ES reported as about 0.04 (not significant). Sentence generation outcome showed an effect large enough to "merit reflections" but was also not significant. Lead author: the result "raises questions about the extensive grammar specifications in England's national curriculum." — [UCL IOE news, Mar 2022](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/ioe/news/2022/mar/what-extent-does-grammar-teaching-help-children-learn-write); [University of York news 2022](https://www.york.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2022/research/grammar-teaching-school/); [Nuffield Foundation report PDF](https://www.nuffieldfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Grammar-and-Writing-in-Englands-National-Curriculum-Report.pdf); [JoWR article page](https://www.jowr.org/jowr/article/view/1346); [UCL Discovery record](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10219086/). Note: a 0.15 (p = 0.21) figure also appeared in snippets for an unspecified outcome; not relied on.
- **EEF Grammar for Writing, first trial (Torgerson et al. 2014, University of York)**, Year 6, England: whole-class delivery over four weeks ES = 0.10 (not significant, 95% CI -0.10 to 0.31); EEF concluded it is not effective for general writing as a whole-class intervention. Small-group delivery was "modestly effective," likely because of small-group teaching rather than the programme itself (secondary source: ES 0.24, +0.78 marks of 32). — [EEF project page, first trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/grammar-for-writing); [Torgerson et al. 2014 report (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266967404_Grammar_for_Writing_Evaluation_Report_and_Executive_Summary); [School Psychology Service summary](https://www.theschoolpsychologyservice.com/what-works/grammarforwriting/). Conflict: the Nuffield report cites 0.04 (CI -0.19 to 0.27) for this trial's primary outcome; not resolved.
- **EEF Grammar for Writing, second (effectiveness) trial (Tracey et al., University of York, report 2019)**, whole-class Year 6 over six weeks: "no evidence that Grammar for Writing improves writing attainment for children in Year 6," as measured by the bespoke test. — [EEF project page, second trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/grammar-for-writing-effectiveness-trial); [EEF report PDF, Feb 2019](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/documents/projects/Grammar_for_Writing.pdf?v=1630926383); [Evaluation summary](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/projects/Regrant_-_Grammar_for_Writing_effectiveness.pdf?v=1743417915). Note: the EEF "Grammar for Writing" is the English National Strategy programme, related to but not the same as the Exeter intervention below.

### Inferences
- Even contextualized grammar programmes have mixed replication: Exeter's RCT found 0.21, but EEF's two Year 6 trials and the Englicious Year 2 trial found null whole-class effects. Short durations (4-6 weeks) and young ages may matter. A curriculum should not assume grammar knowledge alone moves composition scores; it needs sustained practice applying it in sentences.
- What distinguishes effective approaches across sources: (a) students manipulate and build sentences (combining, constructing, expanding), (b) grammar is tied to a writing purpose, (c) practice is extended and produces actual writing, (d) for struggling writers, small groups with practice to fluency.

### Gaps
- Hillocks (1986) and Braddock et al. (1963), the classic sources for "grammar doesn't help," were not searched or opened: **unverified**.
- EPPI review's own sentence-combining effect sizes not confirmed.

## 3. Myhill's contextualized "grammar as choice" research

### Takeaway
The Exeter RCT (Jones, Myhill & Bailey 2013) found a positive overall effect of grammar taught in context of writing (ES = 0.21, p < 0.001, 744 Year 8 students, 31 schools), but the benefit went mainly to more able writers and was neutral for below-average writers; a follow-up adapted for weaker writers found 0.33 on sentence structure and punctuation.

### Cited Findings
- **Jones, S., Myhill, D., & Bailey, T. (2013)**, "Grammar for writing? An investigation of the effects of contextualised grammar teaching on students' writing," *Reading and Writing*, 26(8), 1241-1263, DOI [10.1007/s11145-012-9416-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11145-012-9416-1). ESRC funded; mixed methods: RCT plus a qualitative study. Motivation: existing research only considered isolated grammar instruction and offered no theory of how grammar and writing relate instructionally. — [UOW research repository](https://ro.uow.edu.au/sspapers/351); [ProQuest record](https://www.proquest.com/docview/1419161495); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257643292_Grammar_for_writing_An_investigation_of_the_effects_of_contextualised_grammar_teaching_on_students'_writing)
- Sample and result: 744 students, 31 schools in south-west England and the Midlands, blind randomisation; **ES = 0.21, p < 0.001** favouring the intervention. — [search summary of Jones, Myhill & Bailey 2013 / Myhill et al. "Re-thinking grammar"](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Re-thinking-grammar:-the-impact-of-embedded-grammar-Myhill-Jones/91969a47640525e07875696efce3a6c77884636f); [Exeter "Re-thinking Grammar" PDF, Research Papers in Education](https://www.exeter.ac.uk/media/universityofexeter/collegeofsocialsciencesandinternationalstudies/education/research/centres/writing/teacherresources/Re-thinking_Grammar_Research_Papers_in_Education.pdf)
- Differential impact: the intervention benefited **able writers** more (regression modelling); for pupils whose baseline writing was below average the effect was **neutral**. — [Oxford ORA paper "How does playing with grammar impact on pupils' writing?"](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:a07efce0-4365-4176-be11-71f5152961b4/files/mbe3093c399397bd5a1246e979898e474), citing Jones et al. 2013
- Follow-up adapted for weaker Year 8 writers: ES = 0.33 on Sentence Structure and Punctuation (p < 0.05). Separate study from the 0.21 RCT. — [same search summary, Oxford ORA / Exeter sources](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:a07efce0-4365-4176-be11-71f5152961b4/files/mbe3093c399397bd5a1246e979898e474) (exact citation of the follow-up not confirmed)
- Policy impact: Exeter's grammar-for-writing work is documented as shaping policy and practice in England (REF impact case study). — [REF 2014 impact case study](https://impact.ref.ac.uk/casestudies/CaseStudy.aspx?Id=39798)
- A cluster RCT of a similar intervention (Mitchell and colleagues, 2016) and a critique of trials of "grammar for writing" exist. — [ResearchGate: "Experimental trials and 'what works?' in education: The case of grammar for writing"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/321221560_Experimental_trials_and_'what_works'_in_education_The_case_of_grammar_for_writing); [Hudson, "grammar for writing"](https://dickhudson.com/grammar-for-writing/)

### Inferences
- Myhill's approach (grammar named in the moment it serves a writing choice, with discussion of effects) is the closest evidence-based model to a "grammar symbols linked to meaning" curriculum. But the finding that weaker writers did not benefit is a direct caution for a curriculum aimed first at neurodivergent and disabled learners: those learners appear to need more explicit, scaffolded, fluency-building sentence work (see Q4) before or alongside "choice" talk.
- This supports a sequence of explicit sentence construction first, then rhetorical choice: the curriculum's stated premise.

### Gaps
- Myhill's specific pedagogical principles (e.g., the "LEAD" principles: Links, Examples, Authentic texts, Discussion) were not confirmed from any opened source: **unverified**.
- Myhill et al. 2020s work (e.g., Myhill, Lines & Watson on metalinguistic talk) not searched.

## 4. Sentence combining and sentence-construction studies, including for students with writing difficulties

### Takeaway
Peer-assisted sentence combining improved fourth graders' sentence combining, story quality and revising relative to grammar instruction (Saddler & Graham 2005), and Datchuk's Direct Instruction plus fluency-building (precision teaching) sentence-construction interventions produced functional gains in writing complete simple sentences for students with disabilities and writing difficulties; WWC rates teaching sentence construction to fluency as Moderate evidence in the elementary grades.

### Cited Findings
- **Saddler, B., & Graham, S. (2005)**, "The Effects of Peer-Assisted Sentence-Combining Instruction on the Writing Performance of More and Less Skilled Young Writers," *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 97(1), 43-54, DOI 10.1037/0022-0663.97.1.43. 44 fourth graders (skilled and less skilled). Compared with peers receiving grammar instruction, the sentence-combining group became more adept at combining simpler sentences into more complex ones; secondary sources report improved story quality and use of combining when revising, with the advantage holding at a delayed test. — [ERIC EJ688034](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ688034); [ASU Pure record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-effects-of-peer-assisted-sentence-combining-instruction-on-th/); [APS Observer "Fostering Budding Writers"](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/fostering-budding-writers) (story quality and maintenance details are secondary)
- **Saddler, Behforooz & Asaro (2008)**-era work: "The Effects of Peer-Assisted Sentence-Combining Practice on Four Young" writers (single-case). — [ERIC EJ797654 PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ797654.pdf) (title only seen)
- **Saddler & Preschern (2007)**, "Improving Sentence Writing Ability through Sentence-Combining Practice," *TEACHING Exceptional Children*, 39(3). — [SAGE record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/004005990703900301) (title only seen)
- Sentence combining for students with learning disabilities (University of Nebraska-Lincoln special education paper). — [UNL Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1225&context=specedfacpub) (title only seen)
- **Datchuk, S. M. (2016)**, "Writing Simple Sentences and Descriptive Paragraphs: Effects of an Intervention on Adolescents with Writing Difficulties," *Journal of Behavioral Education*, 25, 166-188. Single case, multiple-probe across 4 participants plus pre-post; two phases: sentence instruction with frequency building to a performance criterion, then paragraph instruction. Functional relation between the intervention and gains in accuracy and rate on sentence-construction probes; only "tentative support" for descriptive paragraphs. — [ERIC EJ1100483](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1100483); [Springer DOI 10.1007/s10864-015-9236-x](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10864-015-9236-x)
- **Datchuk, S. M. (2017)**, "A Direct Instruction and Precision Teaching Intervention to Improve the Sentence Construction of Middle School Students With Writing Difficulties," *The Journal of Special Education*, 51(2) (online Aug 2016). Multiple baseline across small groups; three middle school teachers, 15 students with disabilities and writing difficulties. — [SAGE record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0022466916665588); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/307886787_A_Direct_Instruction_and_Precision_Teaching_Intervention_to_Improve_the_Sentence_Construction_of_Middle_School_Students_With_Writing_Difficulties)
- **Datchuk & Kubina (2017)**, *Education and Treatment of Children*, 40(3), 303-326: all participants improved in constructing complete simple sentences per minute. — [CentralReach-hosted PDF](http://centralreach.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2017-Datchuk-and-Kubina.pdf)
- **Datchuk & Kubina (2015)** (review of sentence-level interventions): cites Writing Next's 0.50 sentence-combining effect as applying to students in primary and intermediate grades. — [CentralReach-hosted PDF](https://centralreach.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2015-Datchuk-and-Kubina.pdf)
- Datchuk and colleagues also adapted a sentence intervention for Spanish-speaking language-minority elementary students with writing difficulties, and one with spelling supports. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326799791_A_Sentence_Construction_Intervention_for_Elementary-Aged_Spanish-Speaking_Language-Minority_Students_with_Writing_Difficulties); [ERIC EJ1182861](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1182861.pdf) (titles only seen)
- **WWC, Teaching Elementary School Students to Be Effective Writers (Graham et al., 2012, NCEE 2012-4058)**: Recommendation 3, "Teach students to become fluent with handwriting, spelling, sentence construction, typing, and word processing," rated **Moderate evidence**; sentence-construction intervention groups outperformed comparisons on measures of sentence construction. — [WWC elementary guide PDF](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/practiceguide/writing_pg_062612.pdf); [WWC elementary guides summary](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/WWC_Elementary_Guides.pdf). Conflict: one text excerpt labeled this recommendation Strong; the summary lists Moderate.
- **WWC, Teaching Secondary Students to Write Effectively (Graham et al., NCEE 2017-4002, released Nov 2016, revised Dec 2019)**: three recommendations: explicitly teach and model strategies using a Model-Practice-Reflect cycle; integrate writing and reading (Moderate); use formative assessment (Minimal). Includes a sentence-level revising routine, "CDO" (Compare, Diagnose, Operate). One outcome in the sentence-structure domain was rated inconclusive. — [WWC secondary guide PDF](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/508_WWCPG_SecondaryWriting_122719.pdf); [ERIC ED569984](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED569984); [WWC webinar slides](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/wwc_webinar_swpg_011817.pdf)
- Reading Universe summarizes sentence-combining research for practitioners. — [Reading Universe: What the research says about sentence combining](https://readinguniverse.org/skill-explainer/sentence-writing/sentence-combining-skill-explainer/what-the-research-says-about-sentence-combining) (not opened)

### Inferences
- For learners with disabilities, the strongest sentence-level evidence is single-case: explicit Direct Instruction of a sentence pattern (subject plus predicate, capital letter and punctuation), then timed practice to a fluency criterion. That maps closely onto a symbol-and-formula curriculum: one formula at a time, modeled, practiced to fluency, then combined or expanded.
- Single-case results show gains on the taught sentence skill; transfer to paragraphs or overall quality is weaker ("tentative"). The curriculum should plan explicit bridges from sentence formulas to connected text.
- WWC treats sentence construction as a foundational fluency skill (alongside handwriting and spelling): that is, a prerequisite that frees attention for composing, consistent with "explicit before artistic."

### Gaps
- Hunt (1965) T-unit work and O'Hare (1973) *Sentence Combining: Improving Student Writing Without Formal Grammar Instruction* (NCTE) were not found in search results: **unverified**.
- Saddler (2012), *Teacher's Guide to Effective Sentence Writing* (Guilford): **unverified**, not searched.
- No effect sizes (e.g., Tau-U, NAP) for Datchuk's single-case studies were confirmed.
- No Datchuk article in *Exceptional Children* (2016) was found; the 2016-2017 work is in *Journal of Behavioral Education*, *Journal of Special Education* and *Education and Treatment of Children*.

## 5. Current (2020-2026) studies and reviews

### Takeaway
Recent large trials in England (Englicious, Year 2, reported 2022/2025; EEF Grammar for Writing effectiveness trial, 2019) find no whole-class effect of grammar programmes on overall writing, while a c. 2024 meta-analysis of ~40 years of written-expression interventions for students with learning disabilities includes sentence combining as a category; no 2022-2024 meta-analysis focused on sentence combining was found.

### Cited Findings
- Englicious RCT (2022 Nuffield report; 2025 *Journal of Writing Research* article): no effect on narrative writing in Year 2 (ES about 0.04). — [Nuffield report PDF](https://www.nuffieldfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Grammar-and-Writing-in-Englands-National-Curriculum-Report.pdf); [UCL Discovery 2022 report](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10144257/1/Grammar%20and%20Writing%20in%20Englands%20National%20Curriculum%20Report%20February%202022_.pdf); [JoWR](https://www.jowr.org/jowr/article/view/1346)
- EEF second Grammar for Writing trial (report Feb 2019): no evidence of improvement in Year 6 writing. — [EEF project page](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/grammar-for-writing-effectiveness-trial)
- "A meta-analysis of almost 40 years of research: Unreleasing the power of written expression in students with learning disabilities" (*Educational Research Review*, c. 2024): average effects for 12 intervention categories including sentence-combining instruction; values not seen. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1747938X24000010)
- "Replication of Strategic and Interactive Writing Instruction in a Nationwide Randomized Controlled Trial" (recent, PMC): an explicit strategy-instruction replication. — [PMC12837566](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12837566/) (title only seen)
- A *Reading and Writing* 2021 article (DOI 10.1007/s11145-021-10135-8) appeared in results on writing instruction meta-analysis; content not seen. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11145-021-10135-8) (**unverified**)

### Inferences
- The recent evidence strengthens the older conclusion: knowing grammar (even taught engagingly) does not by itself raise composition scores in short whole-class programmes. Gains appear when instruction is explicit, sustained, sentence-building, and practiced to fluency, with the largest effects for explicit strategy instruction at the text level.

### Gaps
- No full texts opened (network restrictions), so no 2020-2026 effect sizes for sentence-level instruction specifically were confirmed.
- EEF "Improving Literacy in Key Stage 2" guidance and its sentence-combining recommendation were not searched.
