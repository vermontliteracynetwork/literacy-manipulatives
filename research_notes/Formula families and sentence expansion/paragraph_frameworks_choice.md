## 1. Paragraph frameworks and their evidence

### Takeaway
The major U.S. frameworks (The Writing Revolution, Step Up to Writing, IEW, TIDE/TREE under SRSD, PEEL in the UK, Story Grammar Marker for narrative) all share one shape: a fixed slot for the opening or topic sentence, slots for detail or evidence sentences, and a slot for an ending or link. Several of them, like Symbolic Grammar, teach sentence-level skills explicitly before or alongside the paragraph frame. The strongest independent evidence attaches to SRSD (POW + TREE, TIDE) and to the broad category of "text structure instruction". Step Up to Writing, IEW, TWR and Story Grammar Marker rest mostly on vendor, practitioner or general-category evidence, not independent trials of the named program.

### Cited Findings
**The Writing Revolution (Hochman Method)**
- The Single-Paragraph Outline (SPO) is described as "a linear and simple outline". Its official discussion questions ask why a linear layout suits students, how topic and concluding sentences build skills, and how to avoid repeating the topic sentence word for word in the concluding sentence. (search summary, not opened) [The Writing Revolution site](https://thewritingrevolution.org/?p=64186)
- Layout: a topic sentence space at the top, notes for detail sentences in the middle, and a concluding sentence at the bottom. Source is an unofficial secondary page; treat as practitioner description. (search summary, not opened) [Secondary page on the SPO](https://writing-revolution-single-paragraph-outline.pages.dev/)
- Sentence-level work comes first and continues alongside: writing statements, questions, exclamations and commands about a picture, topic or text; kernel sentences (noun plus verb, for example "They built pyramids.") expanded with who, what, when, where, why and how; and "because, but, so" sentence completion, with each conjunction tied to a job ("because" gives a reason). (search summary, not opened) [Goodreads quotes from the book](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/57365255); [Ohio Literacy Academy TWR handout](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Learning-in-Ohio/Literacy/Literacy-Academy/2020-Literacy-Academy/3-09-4-09-Advancing-Thinking-Through-Writing-TWR-ACCESSIBLE-PDF.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US); [Shotton Hall Research School reflection](https://researchschool.org.uk/shottonhall/news/reading-reflection-the-writing-revolution)
- A teacher tips sheet (third-party hosted, not official) says to keep the kernel intact during expansion and answer each question word "in reference to the kernel"; on the Multiple-Paragraph Outline (MPO), the thesis is the only full sentence, students generate support only after being given the thesis, and struggling students go back to an earlier outline. (search summary, not opened) [TWR Tips for TWR Activities](https://multumnonmulta.neocities.org/pdf/TWR%20Tips%20for%20TWR%20Activities.pdf)
- TWR 2.0 is reported to add intermediate steps between the paragraph and the MPO: a Pre-Transition Outline and a Transition Outline, for students who can write paragraphs but are not ready for the MPO. (search summary, not opened; edition details should be checked with the publisher) [TWR site](https://thewritingrevolution.org/?p=63904)
- Gap on evidence: no independent controlled trial of TWR surfaced in this search; see Gaps.

**Step Up to Writing (Voyager Sopris)**
- Color code: green for the topic sentence ("go"), yellow for reasons or facts ("slow down"), red for explanation, examples and evidence ("stop" and explain), and green again for the conclusion. One teacher's notes say that by the end of the year "there should be one red for each yellow." (search summary, not opened) [Apple Education "Stoplight Paragraph"](https://education.apple.com/resource/250013139); [Albany OR Step Up outline](https://albany.k12.or.us/media/2016/04/Step-Up-to-Writing-Outline.pdf); [SlideShare classroom deck](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/step-uptowriting2/9433289)
- Evidence: the publisher hosts a dissertations list and a reviews/evaluations document; no large independent evaluation surfaced. A related peer-reviewed study (Johnson, Chen and Liu, 2016, Learning Disabilities Research & Practice) examined color-coded outlining and color-coded paragraph writing for students with learning disabilities, but its results were not visible in the summary. (search summary, not opened) [Voyager Sopris dissertations](https://www.voyagersopris.com/docs/default-source/literacy/step-up-to-writing/sutw-dissertations.pdf); [Voyager Sopris reviews/evaluations](https://www.voyagersopris.com/docs/default-source/literacy/step-up-to-writing/sutw_reviews_evaluations.pdf)

**IEW Structure and Style**
- Units are sequenced: Unit 1 key word outlines (main idea of each source sentence captured as key words), Unit 2 writing a summary from the outline, Unit 3 retelling a three-paragraph story by answering Story Sequence Chart questions instead of pulling key words. (search summary, not opened) [IEW Units 1-2 PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/iew-public-production/files/page/fileattachment/Units_1-2_Key-Word_Outlines_and_Summaries.pdf); [IEW "Application for All Nine Units"](https://iew.com/sites/default/files/page/fileattachment/Application_for_All_Nine_of_IEW_Units.pdf)
- Style elements are added one at a time ("EZ+1"): -ly adverb, then who/which clause, then strong verb, and eventually six dress-ups and six sentence openers in every paragraph; "decorations" and advanced openers come later. Sentence openers are words, phrases or clauses placed before the main clause. (search summary, not opened) [IEW blog, reluctant writers and style](https://iew.com/support/blog/jelping-reluctant-writers-with-style); [IEW blog, perfecting style](https://iew.com/support/blog/perfecting-style-time-practice); [IEW blog, decorations](https://iew.com/support/blog/delving-into-decorations-iew-style)
- A widely shared "3 key words max per sentence" rule comes from a third-party checklist based on IEW, not confirmed as official. (search summary, not opened) [Third-party IEW master checklists](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/57c32e32d482e921b76e77a4/t/64e60a3fe620cc344b43e4e3/1692797504391/IEW+Master+Checklists.pdf)
- No independent outcome study of IEW surfaced.

**SRSD mnemonics (POW + TREE, TIDE)**
- TIDE is given as Topic sentence, Important evidence, Detail, Ending (some versions differ), with an ending that ties back to the topic via a generalization, outcome or call to action; one rubric scores each part 0 to 2. (search summary, not opened) [Writing Architect TIDE resources](https://writingarchitect2.azurewebsites.net/tideResources.html); [Think SRSD: How to Get Started](https://scienceofreadingclassroom.substack.com/p/think-srsd-how-to-get-started)
- Autism evidence for POW + TREE (persuasive): Asaro-Saddler and Bak (2012, Topics in Language Disorders), three 8 to 9 year olds with ASD, posttest above baseline, and special education teachers implemented with fidelity after limited training. (search summary, not opened) [SUNY Research Connect record](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/teaching-children-with-high-functioning-autism-spectrum-disorders/)
- Bronaugh (2013, George Mason dissertation), multiple baseline with six students with high-functioning autism (grades 4 and 6), reported 88.83 to 100% PND at post-SRSD on essay measures. (search summary, not opened) [GMU MARS](https://mars.gmu.edu/handle/1920/8191)
- Melogno and colleagues (2020, Brain Sciences): one adolescent boy with ASD, POW + TREE, clear gains comparing three baseline and three post essays. (search summary, not opened) [MDPI Brain Sciences 10(5):264](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/10/5/264)
- Asaro-Saddler (2016) reviewed 11 SRSD studies with learners with ASD: gains in planning, number of written elements and content. (search summary, not opened; reported via the Melogno article) [PMC7288043](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7288043)
- Autism CRC (Australia) compared handwriting, writing-support software and SRSD with POW + TREE, teaching the strategy via video modelling. (search summary, not opened) [Autism CRC executive summary](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/sites/default/files/inline-files/2.010%20Overcoming%20difficulties%20with%20written%20expression%20Executive%20Summary%20FINAL%20WEB.pdf)

**PEEL / PEE (UK)**
- Most UK university guides expand PEEL as Point, Evidence, Explanation, Link; others use Evaluation or Expansion for the second E (Ulster: "POINT EVIDENCE EXPANSION LINK"). Staffordshire adds a fifth step (PEELC, a critical point). Guides repeatedly warn against listing evidence without commentary; the Link either connects back to the question or forward to the next paragraph. (search summary, not opened) [Staffordshire PEEL guide](https://libguides.staffs.ac.uk/academic_writing/PEEL); [Ulster essay guide](https://www.ulster.ac.uk/student/success/academic/academic-content/writing-essays/writing-critically-and-structuring-your-essay); [TUS PEEL sentence starters](https://tus.ie/app/uploads/PEEL-Paragraph-Sentence-Starters.pdf)
- No outcome research on PEEL as a named framework surfaced; it appears as study-skills guidance.

**Story Grammar Marker (MindWing Concepts)**
- Uses icons for story parts (character, setting, problem/initiating event, plan, attempts, outcome), described as a visual, tactile, kinesthetic tool. (search summary, not opened) [Boom Learning blog on narrative development](https://blog.boomlearning.com/what-is-narrative-development)
- The specific evidence is vendor-affiliated: an ASHA poster (Lafontaine and Moreau, one author at MindWing) with 28 school-age children with language impairment reports effectiveness for listening comprehension and oral expression. Poster, conflict of interest, not peer reviewed. (search summary, not opened) [MindWing ASHA poster](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0853/7592/files/mindWing_ASHAposterFinal.pdf)
- A 2020 LSHSS tutorial lists Story Grammar Marker among U.S. narrative intervention approaches and reports that effect sizes for narrative intervention in general have been large for children with and without language disorder (category evidence, not product evidence). (search summary, not opened) [LSHSS 2020 tutorial](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/full/10.1044/2020_LSHSS-20-00015)

**Hamburger paragraph**
- No research or authoritative program source was found in this session; it appears only as common classroom practice. See Gaps.

### Inferences
- Every framework above has three to five fixed slots. That makes a "paragraph formula" in Symbolic Grammar consistent with mainstream practice: each slot can name a recommended sentence formula (for example, topic sentence slot = a statement formula; detail slots = statement formulas expanded with because/but/so or with when/where phrases; ending slot = a statement that restates in new words, or for opinion a "should" statement).
- TWR's kernel expansion and "because, but, so" are the closest existing analog to her sentence formulas feeding paragraph parts; IEW's one-at-a-time "EZ+1" addition of sentence openers and dress-ups is a model for adding one new formula per paragraph rather than many at once.
- The strongest evidence base for neurodivergent learners specifically is SRSD (explicit mnemonic plus modeling plus self-regulation), so pairing each paragraph formula with SRSD-style self-talk and a checklist is better supported than the color code or icon alone.

### Gaps
- No independent RCT or quasi-experiment for TWR, Step Up to Writing, IEW, PEEL or Story Grammar Marker as named programs surfaced in searches; journal hosts were unreachable to check further.
- Hamburger paragraph: no source found.
- Findings of Johnson, Chen and Liu (2016) on color-coded paragraph writing were not visible.

## 2. Research on text structure instruction, planning frames, graphic organizers, and transitions/cohesion

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses support explicit text structure instruction (ES about 0.59 in grades 1 to 5) and, more strongly, strategy instruction for planning (ES about 0.82 to 1.02). Graphic organizers show moderate to large effects in single-case writing research with students with learning disabilities, but autism-specific written-narrative evidence for graphic organizers is thin. Connectives (transition words) matter for cohesion, but research flags overuse and misuse, not only absence.

### Cited Findings
- Graham, McKeown, Kiuhara and Harris (2012), grades 1 to 5: strategy instruction ES = 1.02, text structure instruction ES = 0.59, transcription ES = 0.55, creativity/imagery ES = 0.70; adding self-regulation to strategy instruction ES = 0.50. (search summary, not opened; the 0.59 figure is repeated by Koster et al.'s review) [ERIC ED659639](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED659639.pdf); [Writing Approaches in Years 3 to 13 Evidence Review](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/259618/1/Writing_Approaches_in_Years_3_to_13_Evidence_Review.pdf)
- Graham and Perin (2007, Writing Next), adolescents: strategy instruction (planning, revising, editing) ES = 0.82 overall (n = 20), 1.02 for low-achieving students (n = 9). (search summary, not opened) [Oregon ODE Writing Next synopsis](https://www.oregon.gov/ode/educator-resources/essentialskills/Documents/essentialskillwriting_hs_level2_writing_next_synopsis.pdf)
- Graham, Harris and Santangelo (2015, Elementary School Journal) is a separate meta-analysis and meta-synthesis of evidence-based writing practices; Graham and Harris (2016, The Reading Teacher, "A Path to Better Writing") summarizes it for teachers. (search summary, not opened) [A Path to Better Writing PDF](https://edusites.uregina.ca/nicolemccracken/wp-content/uploads/sites/327/2022/05/A-Path-to-Better-Writing.pdf)
- Collins et al. (2024) IES-funded K-5 meta-analysis: one source reports 378 studies and 3,727 effect sizes, another says 359 studies (conflict unresolved). (search summary, not opened) [IES award page](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/comprehensive-meta-analysis-writing-interventions-students-grades-k-5)
- WWC practice guide "Teaching Elementary School Students to Be Effective Writers" (NCEE 2012-4058): four recommendations covering daily writing time, teaching the writing process and strategies, fluent basic skills (including sentence construction), and a supportive writing community; Recommendation 1 is rated "Minimal" evidence. Text structure appears as a feature illustrated by exemplary texts. Recommendation numbering conflicted across summaries. (search summary, not opened) [WWC Practice Guide 17](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/17); [Nebraska DOE Recommendation 1 PDF](https://www.education.ne.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Teaching-Elementary-School-Students-to-be-Effective-Writers-Recommendation-1.pdf)
- Boon, Barbetta and Paal (2018) synthesized single-case studies of graphic organizers on writing outcomes for students with learning disabilities; a 2024 meta-analysis of graphic organizers for K-12 students with disabilities also draws on single-case studies; story-map graphic organizers show moderate to high effects for students with LD in reading. (search summary, not opened) [Taylor & Francis 2024 article](https://tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09362835.2024.2389080)
- A 2025 meta-analysis of pictorial/graphic representations for K-12 students with ASD (five single-case studies, reading comprehension) cautions that simple "beginning, middle, end" frames may not give enough cognitive challenge for inferencing. (search summary, not opened) [Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-025-07014-4)
- Stringfield, Luscre and Gast (2011): story map single-case study raised reading test scores for students with high-functioning autism (reading, not writing). (search summary, not opened) [NCAEP record](https://ncaep.fpg.unc.edu/resource/effects-of-a-story-map-on-accelerated-reader-postreading-test-scores-in-students-with-high-functioning-autism/)
- A 2025 MDPI single-case multiple-baseline study taught story grammar icons explicitly to four autistic children (ages 7 and 9) within therapy, measuring oral and reading retells. (search summary, not opened) [MDPI Behavioral Sciences 15(8):1020](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/15/8/1020)
- Transitions: Reading Rockets suggests combining simple sentences with transitions and asking whether a piece can have too many transitions. (search summary, not opened) [Reading Rockets, Transition Words](https://www.readingrockets.org/classroom/classroom-strategies/transition-words)
- Research on L2 and adolescent writers reports that students overuse and misuse connectives; one adolescent argumentative-writing study links connective use to argument sophistication. (search summary, not opened; abstracts only) [IAFOR 2025 paper](https://papers.iafor.org/wp-content/uploads/papers/iice2025/IICE2025_88234.pdf?t=8); [eScholarship paper](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6414z5pr/qt6414z5pr.pdf)

### Inferences
- A paragraph-formula tool should teach the structure (text structure, ES about 0.59) and also a planning routine with self-regulation (strategy instruction, ES about 1.0); the latter is the bigger lever.
- Transition words should be taught by function (time order, reason, contrast, adding) and attached to a slot, rather than given as a long list, which matches the overuse findings. TWR's transition outlines are a model for this.
- For autistic students, the frame should not stop at beginning/middle/end; story grammar parts (problem, plan, attempt, outcome) give more to reason about.

### Gaps
- No autism-specific study of graphic organizers for written narratives surfaced.
- No direct experimental study of explicit transition-word instruction for elementary or neurodivergent writers surfaced.
- Could not open the WWC guide to confirm exact recommendation wording and evidence levels.

## 3. How sentence types map onto paragraph parts

### Takeaway
No program found explicitly assigns grammatical sentence types (statement, question, command, compound) to paragraph slots by rule. Programs instead assign sentence jobs: topic or thesis statement at the top, detail or evidence sentences in the middle, a restating or linking ending. TWR comes closest, teaching sentence types and conjunction-based expansion as feeders for the SPO, and IEW assigns a menu of sentence openers and dress-ups that must appear across a paragraph.

### Cited Findings
- TWR teaches statements, questions, exclamations and commands as separate sentence-level activities, and the SPO slots are topic sentence, detail notes and concluding sentence; official discussion questions focus on writing a concluding sentence that does not copy the topic sentence. (search summary, not opened) [Goodreads TWR quotes](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/57365255); [TWR site](https://thewritingrevolution.org/?p=64186)
- On the MPO, only the thesis is a full sentence; the rest are notes to be expanded later. (search summary, not opened) [TWR Tips sheet](https://multumnonmulta.neocities.org/pdf/TWR%20Tips%20for%20TWR%20Activities.pdf)
- IEW requires, eventually, six dress-ups and six different sentence openers per paragraph, introduced one at a time. (search summary, not opened) [IEW blog](https://iew.com/support/blog/jelping-reluctant-writers-with-style)
- Step Up to Writing maps sentence jobs to colors (green topic and conclusion, yellow reasons, red explanation), with one red per yellow as a target. (search summary, not opened) [Albany OR Step Up outline](https://albany.k12.or.us/media/2016/04/Step-Up-to-Writing-Outline.pdf)
- PEEL maps Point (topic sentence), Evidence, Explanation and Link sentences, and sentence-starter sheets give stems for each slot. (search summary, not opened) [TUS PEEL sentence starters](https://tus.ie/app/uploads/PEEL-Paragraph-Sentence-Starters.pdf)
- TIDE's Ending can be a generalization, outcome or call to action. (search summary, not opened) [Writing Architect TIDE](https://writingarchitect2.azurewebsites.net/tideResources.html)
- IEW Unit 3 builds story paragraphs by answering Story Sequence Chart questions (characters and setting, problem, climax/resolution), so each paragraph of a story is assigned a story-grammar job. (search summary, not opened) [IEW nine units](https://iew.com/sites/default/files/page/fileattachment/Application_for_All_Nine_of_IEW_Units.pdf)

### Inferences
- Symbolic Grammar's paragraph formulas would be distinctive: no surveyed program names a specific grammatical formula per slot. A plausible mapping drawn from these programs: opinion or informational topic slot = statement formula; detail slot = statement expanded with because/but/so (TWR) or a compound sentence; an optional hook = question formula; TIDE-style call-to-action ending = command formula; story opening = statement with who and where; middle = time-order transition plus action statement; dialogue lines = quoted statement, question or exclamation.
- Requiring sentence-opener variety across a paragraph (IEW) could translate to "use at least two different formula codes in this paragraph" once formulas are secure.

### Gaps
- No research was found testing whether assigning grammatical sentence types to specific paragraph slots improves writing.
- No program source on dialogue as a paragraph part was found.

## 4. Choice-based story building: branching narratives, story dice, choice boards, AAC sentence builders

### Takeaway
Interactive fiction and "choose your own adventure" writing are used in classrooms and reviewed in the literature, but evidence is small-scale and descriptive, mostly with older students, and none tests a "pick a sentence, then pick the next" tool for neurodivergent writers. Narrative intervention adapted for AAC users (Story Champs) and aided-AAC grammar work show that structured, symbol-supported storytelling is feasible and promising for children with complex communication needs.

### Cited Findings
- Wright and Weible (2024) systematic review: 63 articles (2012 to 2022) on interactive fiction and text adventures in education; seven themes, including IF as a creative writing medium and for reading literacy. (search summary, not opened) [JRSMTE doi](https://doi.org/10.31756/jrsmte.713)
- Batchelor and Thompson (2022, Middle School Journal): students wrote CYOA stories during remote learning; authors conclude CYOA writing can strengthen writing instruction online and in person. (search summary, not opened) [Miami University CREATE page](https://miamioh.edu/ehs/centers-institutes/create/publications/literacy-innovation-multimodality-digital-composition/digital-choose-your-own-adventure-writing.html)
- Proctor and Blikstein (Stanford) built a web app and curriculum in which students read and write interactive fiction combining text and code literacies. (search summary, not opened) [Stanford interactive fiction resource](https://web.stanford.edu/class/educ211/resources/interactive_fiction.pdf)
- Practitioner voice (Edutopia, ISTE): IF "helps students recognize the different aspects of plot"; students were challenged to give readers several difficult choices; IF is mostly text based, usable at any lexical level. (practitioner voice; search summary, not opened) [Edutopia, Farber](https://www.edutopia.org/blog/new-tools-interactive-fiction-engaged-writing-matthew-farber/); [ISTE](https://iste.org/blog/students-choose-their-own-learning-adventures-with-interactive-fiction)
- Holdstock (2025, Goldsmiths thesis) examines writing IF in secondary English classrooms. (search summary, not opened) [Goldsmiths research online](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/39177/1/EDU_thesis_HoldstockS_2025.pdf)
- KU proof-of-concept study adapted Story Champs (story-grammar-based narrative intervention) for AAC users aged 6 to 12; proficient AAC use was not a prerequisite, and it worked faster with children with lower communication skills. The authors note 60 to 90% of AAC interventions target requests, leaving narrative underserved. (search summary, not opened) [KU Life Span Institute news](https://lifespan.ku.edu/news/article/using-aac-with-storytelling-boosts-literacy-skills-in-proof-of-concept-study)
- 2026 RCT in JSLHR (40 preschoolers): AAC Generative Language Intervention improved aided expressive grammar. (search summary, not opened) [JSLHR 2026](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2026_JSLHR-25-00516)
- Biggs, Carter and Gilson (2018) systematic review of aided AAC modeling for children with complex communication needs. (search summary, not opened) [PubMed 30198767](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30198767/)
- A UEA PhD thesis notes that children who use AAC have limited opportunities to develop narrative compared with peers. (search summary, not opened) [UEA thesis](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/52779/1/PBAILEY_PhD_Thesis_Feb_2015.pdf)

### Inferences
- A decision-tree tool where each next-sentence option is tagged with a story grammar part (setting, problem, plan, attempt, outcome) and a sentence formula code would combine the two best-supported ideas found: story grammar structure (narrative intervention) and explicit sentence formulas. Branch points naturally sit at the problem and attempt slots, which is where IF practitioners say plot awareness is built.
- Limiting choices to two or three per step, with symbol support, mirrors AAC practice and keeps load manageable; a fade path is letting the student write their own option alongside the given ones.
- Because IF evidence is mostly middle school and descriptive, the tool should be treated as an engagement and planning scaffold, measured with her own pre/post retell or writing samples.

### Gaps
- No research found on story cubes/story dice as a writing intervention.
- No study found of picture-sequence choice boards for written (not oral) narratives with autistic students or students with language disorder.
- No study found of a branching "pick the next sentence" tool for any population.

## 5. Criticism of formulaic paragraphs and how frameworks are faded

### Takeaway
The main critique (John Warner, NCTE and composition writers) is that fixed forms like the five-paragraph essay replace choice and thinking with imitation and can become "a straitjacket". Defenders call the form a useful foundation for emerging writers. Programs build in fading: TWR moves from SPO to transition outlines to the MPO and back as needed, IEW layers style one element at a time, and SRSD explicitly moves students from teacher modeling to independent, self-regulated use.

### Cited Findings
- Warner, Why They Can't Write (2018): students follow rules like the five-paragraph essay built to pass high-stakes assessments instead of making choices and thinking; much school writing is "an imitation of writing"; he criticizes rules such as a conclusion that restates the essay ("the audience hasn't forgotten what you've said"). (search summary, not opened) [Project MUSE book page](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/61976); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/55346)
- Pacific Standard, "The Five-Paragraph Essay Must Die". (search summary, not opened) [psmag.com](https://psmag.com/?p=4131)
- Counterpoint: an NCTE blog post says the form can be a strong foundation for emerging writers; Education Week reports scholars warn it can "quickly morph from support to straitjacket". (search summary, not opened) [NCTE blog 2016](https://ncte.org/blog/2016/09/anti-five-paragraph-essay-five-paragraph-essay/); [Education Week 2016](https://edweek.org/teaching-learning/is-the-five-paragraph-essay-history/2016/06)
- Heinemann blog: "why the five-paragraph essay is a problem now and later"; National Writing Project LEAD knowledge base, "considering the 5 paragraph essay"; Berkeley, "Unlearning the Five Paragraph Essay". (search summary, not opened) [Heinemann](https://blog.heinemann.com/why-the-five-paragraph-essay-is-a-problem-now-and-later); [NWP](https://lead.nwp.org/knowledgebase/considering-the-5-paragraph-essay/); [UC Berkeley College Writing](https://writing.berkeley.edu/node/200)
- Fading in TWR: intermediate Pre-Transition and Transition Outlines bridge paragraph to multi-paragraph work, and students who struggle return to an earlier outline. (search summary, not opened) [TWR Tips sheet](https://multumnonmulta.neocities.org/pdf/TWR%20Tips%20for%20TWR%20Activities.pdf); [TWR site](https://thewritingrevolution.org/?p=63904)
- Fading in IEW: EZ+1 adds one technique at a time; advanced openers and decorations are reserved for experienced writers. (search summary, not opened) [IEW blog, perfecting style](https://iew.com/support/blog/perfecting-style-time-practice)
- Step Up to Writing expectations grow over the year (one red per yellow by year's end). (search summary, not opened) [Apple Education Stoplight Paragraph](https://education.apple.com/resource/250013139)
- PEEL guides warn that structure fails when the explanation does not do analytical work (no listing evidence without commentary). (search summary, not opened) [Staffordshire PEEL](https://libguides.staffs.ac.uk/academic_writing/PEEL)

### Inferences
- The critique targets fixed counts and restating conclusions used as an end in themselves. Symbolic Grammar can answer it by presenting paragraph formulas as a starting recipe with explicit "upgrade" steps: vary the formula codes, merge two detail slots, move the topic sentence, end with a question or call to action, and eventually let the student design the paragraph's formula sequence.
- For the decision-tree tool, a fade sequence could be: choose from given sentences, then choose a formula and write the sentence, then write without the tree and label your own sentences with formula codes (self-check, SRSD style).

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found comparing faded versus fixed frameworks, or measuring when students should leave a framework.
- No source found on fading formulas specifically for autistic or language-disordered writers.
