## 1. Plot formulas and story schemas (Propp, story grammar, Save the Cat, Hero's Journey, Vonnegut, Reagan et al., Hallmark)

### Takeaway
Story structure has been formalized again and again, from Propp's 31 ordered functions (1928) to psychologists' story grammars (Stein & Glenn; Mandler & Johnson), screenwriting beat sheets (Snyder, Vogler) and computational arc analysis (Reagan et al. 2016, six basic emotional shapes). The Hallmark Christmas movie is a well-documented modern case of a genre formula that writers and producers describe in beat-like terms, which makes it a teachable, motivating example of "a formula is a recipe of beats in order."

### Cited Findings
**Propp (folktale functions)**
- Propp analyzed 100 Russian folktales from Afanasyev's collection and identified 31 basic structural "functions" — [Wikipedia: Vladimir Propp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Propp) (search summary, not opened).
- Not every tale uses every function, but the order of the functions does not change — [Georgetown course page on Propp](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/lb227/ital489/sem/syllab/propp.htm); [U. Michigan, Rabkin: Propp](https://websites.umich.edu/~esrabkin/Propp.htm) (search summary, not opened; which of these said "ordering must not change" was not separated in the summary).
- The functions are carried out by characters filling seven "spheres of action" (roles); the princess and her father count as one sphere — [Wikipedia: Vladimir Propp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Propp); [York University course page](https://www.yorku.ca/mlc/4319/03-04/clacken/clacken2.html) (search summary, not opened).
- The list begins "1. Absentation, 2. Interdiction" and ends "30. Punishment, 31. Wedding"; functions are sometimes grouped into six stages (preparation, complication, transference, struggle, return, recognition) — [Columbia Literary AI page](https://literaryai.library.columbia.edu/litai/i10/); [U. Michigan, Rabkin](https://websites.umich.edu/~esrabkin/Propp.htm) (search summary, not opened; the six-stage grouping is a later commentators' grouping, wording varied across sources).
- Full English translation of *Morphology of the Folktale* is hosted at [buffalo.edu PDF](https://www.buffalo.edu/content/dam/www/advancement/loyalblues/bookclub/MorphologyoftheFolktale.pdf) (search result, not opened).

**Story grammar research (psychology)**
- Stein & Glenn's story grammar is built from six categories: setting, initiating event, internal response, attempt, consequence, reaction (as described in a study that built stimulus stories from these categories) — [Illinois IDEALS report](https://ideals.illinois.edu/items/17757/bitstreams/63742/data.pdf); [Buffalo history-of-CDS blog: Story Grammar, aka Stein & Glenn (1979)](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/history-of-cds/?p=1395) (search summary, not opened).
- Stein & Glenn's model is described as the most influential 1970s–80s story grammar; later research often mixes categories from several models — [narrative models notes](https://matildacarbo.github.io/narrative_models_export/1979+_storygrammarmodels/storygrammar_notes.html) (search summary, not opened).
- Dating is inconsistent in secondary sources (1977, 1978, 1979) — [ERIC ED209659](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED209659.pdf) (search summary, not opened). The commonly cited chapter is 1979.
- Most story-grammar-based intervention programs follow Stein and Glenn's model — [ASHA LSHSS 2020](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/pdf/10.1044/2020_LSHSS-20-00015?download=true) (search summary, not opened).
- Mandler & Johnson (1977), "Remembrance of things parsed: Story structure and recall," *Cognitive Psychology* 9, 111–151: parsed simple stories with a story grammar; recall omissions and errors could be predicted from the grammar — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/547a84f872977944af1f4a5e024fde5d); [Cambridge, Applied Psycholinguistics follow-up](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/abs/what-makes-a-narrative-memorable/5B7D16F57122E2068F4C7655E34B1922) (search summary, not opened).
- Listeners who heard two interleaved stories tended to recall them in canonical story-grammar order rather than the order heard (Mandler follow-up) — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12333392) (search summary, not opened). Children reorder scrambled paragraphs easily when the story fits their schema — [Illinois IDEALS](https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/17769/bitstreams/63778/data.pdf) (search summary, not opened).
- Counterpoint: one replication reported its results were "not well accounted for by models that stress the importance of story structure as a prime determinant of recall" — [Sage, Psychological Reports 1983](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.2466/pr0.1983.53.3.791) (search summary, not opened).

**Screenwriting formulas**
- Save the Cat: a 15-beat structure from screenwriter Blake Snyder (1957–2009), book *Save the Cat!* (2005) — [Reedsy guide](https://blog.reedsy.com/guide/story-structure/save-the-cat-beat-sheet); [savethecat.com](https://savethecat.com/get-started) (search summary, not opened).
- Beat placements in novel adaptations: Catalyst ~10%, Midpoint ~50%, All Is Lost ~75%; originally page numbers for a ~110-page script; percentages are targets, not rules — [Kindlepreneur](https://kindlepreneur.com/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/); [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/story-structure/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/) (search summary, not opened).
- Hero's Journey: Campbell's monomyth (*The Hero with a Thousand Faces*, 1949) has 17 stages; Christopher Vogler's 12-stage version (*The Writer's Journey*) fits it into three acts and is the one most writing guides use; it begins Ordinary World, Call to Adventure — [Wikipedia: Hero's journey](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Monomyth); [Wikipedia: The Writer's Journey](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/The_Writer%27s_Journey:_Mythic_Structure_For_Writers) (search summary, not opened).
- Vogler's book grew from a seven-page Disney memo, "A Practical Guide to The Hero With a Thousand Faces"; sources disagree on dates (memo 1985 vs. later; book 1992 vs. 2007 edition) — [SlideShare: the memo](https://de.slideshare.net/briansolis/the-memo-that-started-it-all-by-christopher-vogler); [Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/The_Writer%27s_Journey:_Mythic_Structure_for_Writers) (search summary, not opened).

**Vonnegut's story shapes and the 2016 computational study**
- Vonnegut graphed stories on a good fortune / ill fortune axis over time. "Man in Hole": fall then rise ("the story needn't be about a man or a hole"). "Boy Meets Girl": starts in the middle, rises, falls, rises again (finds something, loses it, gets it back). "Cinderella": rise, fall, rise, which he called the most popular story in our civilization — [The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/11/26/kurt-vonnegut-on-the-shapes-of-stories/); [Open Culture](https://openculture.com/2023/02/kurt-vonnegut-diagrams-the-shape-of-all-stories-from-kafkas-metamorphosis-to-cinderella.html); [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/high-culture/vonnegut-shapes/) (search summary, not opened).
- His master's thesis on story shapes was rejected by the University of Chicago (dates given vary, 1946/47 vs 1965); a lecture version is dated 2004 (Case Western Reserve); a written version appears in *A Man Without a Country* — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/literature/authors/kurt-vonneguts-story-diagrams); [Vonnegut lesson PDF, Language Log](https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/myl/VonnegutLessonInCreativeWriting.pdf) (search summary, not opened; dates conflict).
- Reagan, Mitchell, Kiley, Danforth & Dodds (2016), "The emotional arcs of stories are dominated by six basic shapes," *EPJ Data Science* 5, DOI 10.1140/epjds/s13688-016-0093-1. Sentiment analysis with 10,000-word sliding windows over Project Gutenberg fiction; six core arcs found with matrix decomposition (SVD), supervised and unsupervised learning; particular arcs associated with more downloads — [Springer/EPJ DS](https://link.springer.com/10.1140/epjds/s13688-016-0093-1); [arXiv 1606.07772](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07772v3); [author PDF](https://cdanfort.w3.uvm.edu/research/2016-reagan-epj.pdf) (search summary, not opened).
- Corpus size discrepancy: 1,327 stories (arXiv v3 abstract) vs. 1,737 books (v1/appendix) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07772v3); [UVM online appendix](https://www.uvm.edu/storylab/share/papers/reagan2016b/index.html) (search summary, not opened).

**Hallmark / holiday romance as a genre formula (journalism, practitioner voice)**
- A New York Times analysis of Hallmark and Lifetime holiday movies released 2017–2023 found that although plotlines varied, most shared familiar, safe, aspirational tropes such as small towns — [AOL/Yahoo syndication, "What's the recipe for Hallmark and Lifetime holiday movies? - Backstage attributes to that analysis the archetypal storyline: "a recently dumped, high-powered female executive from the city finds new love, purpose, and appreciation for Christmas in a small town with the help of a handsome local fellow" — [Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/best-hallmark-christmas-movies-78065/) (search summary, not opened; attribution to NYT not independently confirmed). This closely matches the curriculum developer's example (bad dating history, moved to a new place, local man).
- Recurring beats named by commentators: someone returns to a small hometown and is drawn to a simpler life; a scene where the town comes together (festival/event); you know within 15 minutes who will fall for whom — [Slashfilm, "10 Things That Happen In Every Hallmark Christmas Movie"](https://www.slashfilm.com/1728759/things-happen-every-hallmark-christmas-movie) (search summary, not opened).
- Holiday movies put the romance first and the "business going under" conflict second (quote from a researcher, Smale) — [Queen's Journal, "Cracking the Hallmark Christmas movie code"](https://www.queensjournal.ca/?p=101592) (search summary, not opened).
- Researchers say the predictability is part of the appeal — [NBC Boston, Why it feels so good to watch Hallmark holiday movies (Pamela Rutledge quoted)](https://www.nbcboston.com/news/national-international/why-it-feels-so-good-to-watch-hallmark-holiday-movies/2004790/?amp=1); business angle: [Ivey HBA blog, "Spinning the Christmas wheel: how Hallmark profits from predictable holiday magic"](https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/hba/blog/2025/01/spinning-the-christmas-wheel-how-hallmark-profits-from-predictable-holiday-magic/) (search summary, not opened).
- Writers' view (AP): the movies seem formulaic but "there's no real checklist"; standard practices include no real villains and a happy ending — [ABC News / AP, "Tips to writing a Christmas TV movie, including no villains and keeping it Christmas-y"](https://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/wireStory/tips-writing-christmas-tv-movie-including-villains-keeping-116771604) (search summary, not opened).
- Save the Cat writer: Hallmark scripts use a "9-act" format (commercial-break blocks each ending on a mini-cliffhanger); map the story on standard three-act beats first, then fit scenes into the blocks — [Save the Cat, "How to Write and Sell a Script to the Hallmark Channel"](https://savethecat.com/tips-and-tactics/how-to-write-and-sell-a-script-to-the-hallmark-channel) (search summary, not opened).
- Producer Carol Baum's template for a holiday rom-com: a bright, ambitious, successful lead who lives in a big city, and a slow-burn, family-friendly romance; Hallmark writer Samantha Herman: the tropes and beats recur "in countless Hallmark movies" and "work more often than not" — [AP via ClickOrlando, "How to write a holiday rom-com for TV, according to the experts" (Dec 2024)](https://www.clickorlando.com/entertainment/2024/12/13/how-to-write-a-holiday-rom-com-for-tv-according-to-the-experts/) (search summary, not opened).
- Exceptions noted: broader diversity in the 2024 lineup; films like *Next Stop, Christmas* where the lead keeps her city career — [Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/best-hallmark-christmas-movies-78065/) (search summary, not opened).

### Inferences
- The developer's Hallmark sequence maps cleanly onto existing frameworks, which gives a ready "same skeleton, different costume" lesson: setting + problem (bad dating history, relocation) = Stein & Glenn setting/initiating event; meeting the "wrong" man = Save the Cat catalyst / debate; festival or charity = fun-and-games / midpoint; falling out = All Is Lost; something brings them back = finale; happily ever after = Propp's function 31 "Wedding" and Vonnegut's Boy Meets Girl shape (rise, fall, rise).
- The beat sheets differ mostly in grain size (6 story grammar categories, 12 Vogler stages, 15 Snyder beats, 31 Propp functions). A curriculum can teach a small core (story grammar's 6) first and present the others as zoomed-in versions.
- Vonnegut's shapes and Reagan's arcs give a visual, nonverbal entry point (a line going up and down) that suits a symbol-based curriculum for neurodivergent learners: plot as a drawn shape before plot as words.

## 2. Evidence for story grammar / narrative structure instruction (LD, autism, DLD; Story Grammar Marker)

### Takeaway
Narrative interventions that directly teach story grammar have moderate-to-large positive effects for school-aged children, including those with language disorders (group-design d ≈ 0.5, single-case d ≈ 1.2 in a 2021 meta-analysis). Children with DLD show their clearest deficits in story grammar (macrostructure), which is why it is a common intervention target. The Story Grammar Marker, the best-known commercial tool, has only vendor-authored evidence.

### Cited Findings
- A 2021 meta-analysis in *Language, Speech, and Hearing Services in Schools* (26 studies) of narrative intervention for school-aged children found overall positive effects: d = 0.51 and 0.54 in group-design studies and d = 1.24 in single-case design studies — [ASHA LSHSS, doi 10.1044/2021_LSHSS-20-00160](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2021_LSHSS-20-00160) (search summary, not opened).
- A companion review hosted by Baylor's LIDS lab included RCTs, quasi-experiments and 16 single-case designs; common intervention features included direct instruction of story grammar and use of authentic children's literature — [Baylor LIDS lab manuscript](https://sites.baylor.edu/lidslab/files/2023/04/InterventionsNarrativeLangauge.MainDocument.REVISED.2.pdf) (search summary, not opened).
- Effect sizes for improving aspects of language in children with and without language disorder through story-grammar-based programs have been large; most such programs follow Stein and Glenn — [ASHA LSHSS 2020](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/pdf/10.1044/2020_LSHSS-20-00015?download=true) (search summary, not opened).
- A 2022 JSLHR meta-analysis (37 studies): children with DLD scored lower than typically developing peers on narrative measures, average −0.82 SD; grammatical accuracy (microstructure) and story grammar (macrostructure) gave the most consistent evidence of group differences; significant heterogeneity — [ASHA JSLHR, doi 10.1044/2022_JSLHR-22-00017](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2022_JSLHR-22-00017) (search summary, not opened).
- Related populations: a 2025 meta-analysis of linguistic interventions for children with hearing loss reported d = 1.01 for macrostructure; a dialogic reading meta-analysis (16 studies) reported g = 0.62 overall and g = 0.82 for macrostructure — [ASHA JSLHR 2025 (24-00456)](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/pdf/10.1044/2025_JSLHR-24-00456?download=true); [ASHA JSLHR 2025 (24-00589)](https://pubs.asha.org/doi/pdf/10.1044/2025_JSLHR-24-00589?download=true) (search summary, not opened; which figure belongs to which paper was not fully separated in the summary).
- **Story Grammar Marker (vendor):** the main study is a 2010 ASHA poster by Lafontaine and Moreau, 28 school-aged children with language impairment, 16 weeks of SGM-based narrative intervention, paired t-tests on listening comprehension and oral expression; no control group or effect sizes visible in excerpts — [MindWing ASHA poster PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0853/7592/files/mindWing_ASHAposterFinal.pdf) (search summary, not opened). **Label: vendor evidence.**
- Developer Maryellen Moreau has ownership interest in MindWing Concepts and holds IP and patents on the SGM (disclosure) — [MindWing SlideShare, "The 6th Pillar of Reading"](https://de.slideshare.net/mindwingconcepts/the-6th-pillar-of-reading) (search summary, not opened).

### Inferences
- Explicit story grammar teaching is one of the better-supported narrative practices for the curriculum's target learners (DLD, LD), and it is the foundation layer on which genre formulas such as the Hallmark pattern can be built.
- Because story grammar is the area where children with DLD differ most, an icon-per-part approach (as SGM and the Symbolic Grammar curriculum both use) addresses a real need, but the specific SGM product's efficacy is not independently established. The general method, not the brand, is what the evidence supports.

### Gaps
- No autism-specific narrative intervention meta-analysis turned up in these searches.
- Whether narrative intervention transfers to *written* original composition (versus oral retell) was not established here.
- Moderator results (dosage, which components matter) for the 2021 meta-analysis were not visible; authors' names not captured from the summary.

## 3. Explicit genre pedagogy: Sydney School Teaching and Learning Cycle, Reading to Learn, Talk for Writing, progymnasmata

### Takeaway
All four traditions share one arc: study a model closely, build a new version together, then write independently. The Sydney School cycle (deconstruction, joint construction, independent construction) and Talk for Writing (imitation, innovation, invention) are direct modern statements of it; the progymnasmata are the ancient version (retell or imitate a fable first, argue last). Evidence is mostly case studies and practitioner reports; the one independent trial found (EEF Talk for Writing pilot) could not securely estimate impact.

### Cited Findings
**Sydney School / SFL Teaching and Learning Cycle**
- The teaching and learning cycle was introduced in the Language and Social Power Project of the Metropolitan East Disadvantaged Schools Program (Martin, 1999), developed from research by Martin & Rothery (1988) comparing the writing teachers expected in the primary years with what students actually produced — [ACU, Fenwick 2022, Teacher use of genre pedagogy](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/download/2e42fb5e661dd0958f254af65a86f5cf9db923bc387a734fe03627c4b8212e98/316773/AM_Fenwick_2022_Teacher_use_of_genre_pedagogy_engaging.pdf) (search summary, not opened).
- It emerged in genre-based literacy approaches in the late 1980s, with an initial emphasis on writing (Rose & Martin, 2012; Rothery, 1994) — [Victoria Department of Education, The teaching-learning cycle](https://www.education.vic.gov.au/school/teachers/teachingresources/discipline/english/literacy/writing/Pages/the-teaching-learning-cycle.aspx) (search summary, not opened).
- Teacher guidance in the first two stages enables the final stage, where students independently construct the genre; joint construction means teacher and students write together — [Western Sydney, The teaching and learning cycle in composing multimodal texts](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/the-teaching-and-learning-cycle-in-composing-multimodal-texts/) (search summary, not opened).
- Primary references: Rothery, *Exploring Literacy in School English* (1994, Metropolitan East DSP); Rose & Martin, *Learning to Write, Reading to Learn: Genre, Knowledge and Pedagogy in the Sydney School* (2012) — [Equinox chapter page](https://www.equinoxpub.com/home/view-chapter/?id=20128) (search summary, not opened).

**Reading to Learn (David Rose)**
- R2L is a genre-based pedagogy built on SFL that teaches reading, and learning from reading, as the basis for writing; developed principally by David Rose, first with Indigenous and other secondary students, then tertiary — [Universitat de Barcelona, Didacticae article](https://revistes.ub.edu/index.php/didacticae/article/download/42427/40441/121724) (search summary, not opened).
- Case study (Spanish high-stakes exam setting): planned reading support and text annotation had a positive impact on joint construction of the less familiar argumentative genre; teachers could implement it with minimal professional development — [Sheffield Hallam, Moore, Reading to Learn (accepted manuscript)](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/34619/3/Moore-ReadingToLearn%28AM%29.pdf) (search summary, not opened; which of these two sources reported the Spanish case was not clearly separated).
- A review of the 2026 volume *Reading to Learn, Reading the World* reports similar results across a whole Brisbane school (Carusi-Lees), and that at one institution that dropped the program, gains reversed after returning to traditional teaching — [UTS Literacy and Numeracy Studies review](https://epress.lib.uts.edu.au/journals/index.php/lnj/article/view/1949/2097) (search summary, not opened).
- A 2012 NSW government evaluation of Reading to Learn exists; its findings were not visible — [NSW CESE evaluation PDF](https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/about-us/educational-data/cese/evaluation-evidence-bank/2012-np-literacy-and-numeracy-program-evaluation-of-reading-to-learn.pdf) (search result, not opened).

**Talk for Writing (Pie Corbett)**
- Three stages: imitation (pupils learn and internalize a text, often orally), innovation (co-construct new versions with the teacher), invention (create original texts independently) — [EEF, Talk for Writing pilot](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/talk-for-writing) (search summary, not opened).
- Created for primary schools by Pie Corbett and adapted for secondary by Julia Strong — [Oxford ORA thesis](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:9ea4ff1b-99e3-480f-918a-f1f9f3fccf06/files/dw0892b60b) (search summary, not opened).
- EEF pilot, evaluated by UCL Institute of Education: six of ten intervention schools (one withdrew) plus three comparison schools over twelve months. It "was not able to securely estimate the impact of the programme on academic attainment," and prior research gives "a mixed amount of support" for the underlying principles — [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/talk-for-writing) (search summary, not opened).
- Secondary claim: the 2013–14 Portsmouth pilot reported teacher enthusiasm, increased confidence teaching writing, and feasibility — [Suffolk County Council PDF](https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/asset-library/talk-for-writing.pdf) (search summary, not opened; not confirmed against EEF).
- EEF's writing research agenda says new evaluation findings are expected from Spring 2026 onward — [EEF research agenda: Writing](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/research-agenda-themes-priority-areas/research-agenda-theme-writing) (search summary, not opened). Whether they have been published by Nov 2026 was not checked.

**Progymnasmata (classical graded exercises)**
- Students took a fable (usually Aesop's) and expanded it, condensed it, or wrote a new fable in close imitation; teachers recommended converting indirect to direct speech; Aphthonius put fable first; the exercises ran from storytelling to building an argument in order of difficulty — [BYU Silva Rhetoricae: Fable](https://rhetoric.byu.edu/Pedagogy/Progymnasmata/Fable.htm); [Wikipedia: Progymnasmata](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Progymnasmata); [Kennedy, Progymnasmata translations (SBL PDF)](https://www.sbl-site.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/KennedyProgymnasmata.pdf) (search summary, not opened).
- Modern use: Classical Academic Press's *Writing & Rhetoric* series, 12 books over six years from grades 3–4, starting with Fable, then narrative, then chreia and proverb, then persuasive and comparative essays, using whole-story reading and imitation — [Writing & Rhetoric scope and sequence PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0264/3014/4583/files/WR_Scope_and_Sequence.pdf) (search summary, not opened). **Label: publisher material.** James Selby's *Classical Composition* adapts the Quintilian/Cicero sequence for grades 4–12 — [Veritas Press PDF](https://store.veritaspress.com/site/assets/145315.pdf) (search summary, not opened; publisher).

### Inferences
- The three modern cycles line up one to one: Sydney School deconstruction = Talk for Writing imitation = progymnasmata retell/imitate a fable; joint construction = innovation = expand/condense/alter a fable together; independent construction = invention = write a new fable. This gives the curriculum a well-pedigreed name for each step of "formula to composition."
- Applied to the Hallmark example: deconstruct (label the beats in one movie or picture book), joint construction (class swaps the costume: a new setting and a different festival on the same beats), independent construction (each student writes their own, possibly bending one beat on purpose).
- The evidence base is weaker than the narrative intervention literature: mostly case studies, practitioner and publisher reports, and one inconclusive pilot. Claims should say "widely used, promising" rather than "proven."

### Gaps
- No effect sizes found for Reading to Learn or the Teaching and Learning Cycle; the NSW 2012 evaluation findings were not visible.
- No outcome research found on modern progymnasmata curricula.
- No study found that measures the *originality* of student writing after these cycles (only attainment or quality).

## 4. Formula and invention: genre conventions as tools, subversion, constraint-based creativity, and whether structure helps or limits originality

### Takeaway
The creativity research cuts both ways: seeing examples reliably pulls new work toward the examples' features (the "conformity effect"), but copying or working from examples can also raise creativity depending on the kind of example and what students do with it. Oulipo shows that explicit, deliberate constraints can be generative. The practical lesson is to teach the formula explicitly, then build in a step where students change it on purpose, rather than leaving models as the last thing students see.

### Cited Findings
- Smith, Ward & Schumacher (1993), "Constraining effects of examples in a creative generation task," *Memory & Cognition*: across three experiments, people who saw examples produced designs more likely to contain the examples' features than controls; recent experience can cause unintentional conformity; the effect weakened when an unrelated task was placed between seeing examples and generating — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/bf03202751.pdf); [Joel Chan notes on the paper](https://publish.obsidian.md/joelchan-notes/discourse-graph/sources/%40smithConstrainingEffectsExamples1993) (search summary, not opened).
- The conformity effect is described as very robust, even when the shared features were chosen arbitrarily (Marsh and colleagues) — [Springer, Memory & Cognition](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758%2FBF03211419.pdf) (search summary, not opened).
- Okada & Ishibashi (2017), *Cognitive Science*: copying abstract artworks increased the creativity of participants' own drawings; whether examples constrain or help depends on the example type — [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9n14h1bp/qt9n14h1bp.pdf?t=mtfdje) (search summary, not opened; the search summary attributed this, but which link holds it was not confirmed).
- Conformity to example features was greatest when people believed the examples came from highly credible sources — [UGA thesis, Cook 2002](https://getd.libs.uga.edu/pdfs/cook_gabriel_i_200212_ms.pdf) (search summary, not opened).
- Oulipo (Ouvroir de littérature potentielle), founded 1960 by French mathematicians and writers, to find new forms of writing through numerical and alphabetical constraints — [Wikipedia: Oulipo](https://Wikipedia.com/wiki/Oulipo) (search summary, not opened).

### Inferences
- Explicit formula teaching risks the conformity effect if the model is the last thing students see before writing. Two research-consistent mitigations: (a) a gap or different task between studying the model and inventing (Smith et al.'s delay weakened conformity), and (b) asking students to abstract the structure (beats, shapes, symbols) rather than copy surface details (Okada & Ishibashi's abstract-example finding). Teaching the formula as labeled beats or symbols is itself a form of abstraction away from surface features.
- An Oulipo-style "change one rule on purpose" step (e.g., the festival brings them apart instead of together; the man is right and the woman is wrong; tell it as Man in Hole instead of Boy Meets Girl) gives a concrete, teachable route from formula to artistic choice, and is consistent with the invention stage of Talk for Writing.
- Labeling the conventions ("this is the falling-out beat") makes the invisible rules visible, which Oulipo commentators identify as the precondition for bending them deliberately.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found that tests whether explicit genre/plot structure teaching increases or decreases the *originality* of children's written stories. This is the key open question and the claims above are extrapolations from design/drawing tasks and adult writing practice.
- Stokes, *Creativity from Constraints*, was searched but did not appear in results (author first name, year and publisher unconfirmed); not cited.
- Author interviews explicitly on "you have to know the rules before you break them" (e.g., from named novelists) were not found in this pass; no quotes are given to avoid misattribution.
- Research on subversion/parody as a classroom genre move (e.g., fractured fairy tales) was not searched.
