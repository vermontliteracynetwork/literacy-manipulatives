# Visual and Predictable Writing-Instruction Resources (teacher-made, commercial, clinical)

Scope: resources that teach sentences, paragraphs and stories through visuals and predictable patterns, for the Symbolic Grammar curriculum (grammar symbols, sentence formulas, explicit-first, neurodivergent and disabled learners).

Method note: this session's network blocks many hosts. Every finding below comes from web search result summaries. **No page was opened (all items are "search summary, not opened")** unless stated otherwise. Labels: [PRACTITIONER] = teacher, school or blog voice; [VENDOR] = seller or publisher claim; [CLINICAL] = SLP/NHS/therapy provider; [RESEARCH] = peer-reviewed or thesis. Teachers Pay Teachers, Pinterest, TikTok and Facebook pages did not show up in search results, so those platforms are covered only indirectly (see Gaps).

## 1. Sentence-building resources (builders, scrambles, color-coded strips, silly sentences, apps, AAC boards, expansion charts)

### Takeaway
The strongest precedent for Symbolic Grammar comes from UK speech-and-language systems that color- or shape-code sentence parts: Colourful Semantics (color = meaning role: who / doing / what / where) and Shape Coding (shapes = syntax, colors = word class, arrows = tense). Around them sits a large teacher-made and commercial ecosystem of color-coded strips, scrambles, "silly sentences" and builder apps. These ideas are well established and popular, but the formal outcome evidence is thin: small clinical studies and vendor claims.

### Cited Findings
**Clinical color/shape systems (closest analogues to grammar symbols)**
- Colourful Semantics (Alison Bryan) color-codes sentence structure (syntax) and links each color to meaning (semantics). It is used mainly with children with speech, language and communication needs. [CLINICAL] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Leicestershire Partnership NHS](https://www.leicspart.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Colourful-Semantics.pdf); [Just One Norfolk NHS](https://www.justonenorfolk.nhs.uk/speech-and-language/using-sentences/colourful-semantics/); [Alison Bryan intro, Integrated Treatment Services](https://integratedtreatmentservices.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/An-introduction-to-Colourful-Semantics-Alison-Bryan.pdf)
- The color schemes **differ by provider**. One uses orange = who, yellow = what doing, green = what. Another uses orange = who, yellow = doing what, blue = where. A third keys colors to word type (verbs orange, nouns yellow or green). (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Bradford info sheet](https://bso.bradford.gov.uk/userfiles/file/!Learning%20Support/Colourful%20Semantics%20-%20Info%20and%20Resources.pdf); [Lancashire STARS](https://www.lancashire.gov.uk/media/961039/stars-colourful-semantics.pdf); [Twinkl](https://www.twinkl.com/resources/language-speech-pathology-australia/expressive-language-language-speech-pathology-australia/semantics-expressive-language-language-speech-pathology-australia)
- The teaching sequence starts with two-element sentences (who + doing) and adds roles one at a time. Every sentence must include a verb. (search summary, not opened). Source: [Structural Learning guide](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/colourful-semantics-a-teachers-guide)
- Evidence caveat: one guide notes that the main clinical group study involved only six children (Bolderson et al., 2011). (search summary, not opened, secondary source). Source: [Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/colourful-semantics-a-teachers-guide)
- Shape Coding (Dr Susan Ebbels, Moor House School) teaches spoken and written grammar to school-aged children with Developmental Language Disorder. It uses shapes for syntactic structures, colors for word classes, arrows for tense and aspect, and lines for gender and plurals. [CLINICAL] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Moor House School](https://moorhouseschool.co.uk/shape-coding); [Carrington Infant School handout](https://www.carrington-inf.bucks.sch.uk/_site/data/files/migrated/shape-coding/shape-coding.pdf)
- Ebbels's paper notes that shapes can nest inside each other to show hierarchical structure and can be moved around, which color-only systems cannot do. [RESEARCH] (search summary, not opened). Source: [Ebbels, UCL Discovery accepted version](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1516142/1/Ebbels_Ebbels%20Shape%20Coding%20therapy%20(accepted%20version)_segment.pdf)
- Some providers start children on Colourful Semantics and move them to Shape Coding for more complex grammar. A secondary site cites a randomised trial on past tense (Calder et al., 2020) that this search could not verify, and sources disagree on whether Shape Coding dates from 2001 or 2007. (search summary, not opened). Source: [Structural Learning comparison](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/colourful-semantics-vs-shape-coding)

Sources: [TWR because-but-so](https://www.thewritingrevolution.org/strategy/because-but-so); [Substack, TWR in math class](https://fivetwelvethirteen.substack.com/p/the-writing-revolution-in-math-class)

### Inferences
- Symbolic Grammar's symbol-plus-formula approach has clear precedent in Shape Coding, which already uses arrows for tense and colors for word class. It is the most important comparator to study in depth, both to avoid accidentally duplicating it and to borrow evidence-informed design choices such as nesting shapes for phrases.
- Because Colourful Semantics schemes conflict across providers, a single fixed and published Symbolic Grammar key (one symbol and one color per job, never reused) would be a real differentiator for students who move between settings.
- Kernel expansion (TWR and Reading Universe) maps directly onto formula notation: kernel = base formula, each question card = an optional slot symbol. A "who / did what / when / where / why" card deck could be keyed to the existing symbols.
- Builder apps (Clicker Sentence Sets, SentenceBuilder) show that the "tap words in a grid / spin a word wheel" interaction works on iPad. The reviewer complaint about dullness suggests game-like motion and choice matter.

### Gaps
- No Teachers Pay Teachers listings showed up in search results, so specific TpT sentence-builder, sentence-cube, word-wheel or sentence-dice products were not inventoried. A direct TpT search is needed.
- No sources were found for Mad Libs-style classroom grammar activities, sentence dice or cubes, or word-wheel paper sentence makers. These were not searched separately because of the tool budget.
- No independent outcome studies were found for Clicker, SentenceBuilder or the color-coded commercial products.
- The Calder et al. (2020) Shape Coding trial and the Bolderson et al. (2011) Colourful Semantics study were not opened, so their samples and effect sizes are unverified.

## 2. Predictable and pattern texts (pattern books, predictable chart writing, innovating on a pattern, research)

### Takeaway
Pattern books such as Brown Bear are widely used as writing models: classes "innovate" by swapping words into the fixed frame. Predictable Chart Writing (Hanser, Erickson; UNC CLDS) is the best-documented predictable-writing routine for students with significant disabilities and AAC users. Using predictable texts for *reading* is contested in the science-of-reading debate, so their best use in Symbolic Grammar is as writing and syntax frames, not as decoding texts.

### Cited Findings
- Predictable books use patterns, sequences and picture-word connections so children can guess "what comes next". [PRACTITIONER] (search summary, not opened). Source: [Carol Hurst, First Books for Emergent Readers](https://carolhurst.com/profsubjects/reading/emergentreaders.html)
- Criticism: Reading Rockets (Goldberg) argues that children who struggle with the code can spend years with predictable books, treating reading as guessing. The Conversation says their features do not match the letter-sound correspondences taught in phonics, and that research on beginning-reading texts is sparse. Sources: [Reading Rockets blog](https://www.readingrockets.org/blogs/right-to-read/predictable-books-purpose-written-guessing); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/explainer-whats-the-difference-between-decodable-and-predictable-books-and-when-should-they-be-used-106531) (search summary, not opened)
- Counterpoint: Brown (1999, *The Reading Teacher*) calls the choice between decodable and predictable texts a false choice and says simple predictable texts build awareness of how books work. Kratky (a vendor guide) describes patterned readers as "message driven" and decodables as "phonics driven". Sources: [Brown 1999 PDF, Utah](https://uurc.utah.edu/_resources/documents/research/brown-readtchr-534-1999dc.pdf); [Kratky, Cengage](https://ngl.cengage.com/assets/downloads/pan_pro000009040/am_kratky_patterned_texts.pdf) (search summary, not opened)
- A Michigan State dissertation compared repeated reading of pattern books with child-dictated writing in Head Start. Its findings were not in the excerpt. Source: [MSU dissertation](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/31254/OBJ/download) (search result only)
- Innovating on Brown Bear: lesson plans say the predictable text makes it easy for children to write their own book. The stem "I see ___ looking at me" lets writers substitute their own words, and the pattern does not need to match the book exactly. One guide suggests a per-page checklist: complete sentence, supporting picture, clear narration. Georgia pre-K guides add laminated character cutouts for sequencing and pocket-chart sentence strips. [PRACTITIONER] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Creative Educator, Adapt a Pattern Story](https://recipes.tech4learning.com/2017/lessons/adapt-a-pattern-story); [Education.com, Make a Patterned Story](https://www.education.com/activity/article/make_a_patterned_story); [Georgia DECAL Brown Bear](https://www.decal.ga.gov/documents/attachments/BrownBear04-05.pdf); [Victoria (AU) literacy toolkit, Brown Bear](https://www.vic.gov.au/literacy-teaching-toolkit-early-childhood/experience-plans/brown-bear)
- A Routledge/Stenhouse title, *Patterns of Wonder, Grades PreK-1: Inviting Emergent Writers to Play with...* (La Rocca and Anderson), turned up in results (title only). Source: [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Patterns-of-Wonder-Grades-PreK-1-Inviting-Emergent-Writers-to-Play-with/La-Rocca-Anderson/p/book/9781625314505)
- Predictable Chart Writing is a shared group writing routine that produces a class book. The teacher writes a title and a sentence stem, students brainstorm, and each student completes the stem with their own word. It supports emergent and conventional writers and is one of five daily emergent-literacy routines (Erickson 2017). [RESEARCH CENTER/PRACTITIONER] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Paths to Literacy, Predictable Chart Writing 2021](https://pathstoliteracy.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Predictable_Chart_Writing_2021.pdf); [Angelman Syndrome Foundation handout](https://www.angelman.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Predictable-chart-writing-012815.pdf); [Musselwhite handout, USU](https://cehs.usu.edu/comdde/files/summer-seminars/Musselwhite_Handout_6_slides.pdf)
- Adapted versions serve students who cannot speak or hold a pencil. Hanser's principle is that no one needs to be "ready" for comprehensive emergent literacy instruction. Handouts cite Erickson and Koppenhaver (2020), *Comprehensive Literacy for All* (Brookes), as the primary source; the book was not retrieved. (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Iowa DOE](https://educate.iowa.gov/media/5960/download); [Sheldon and Erickson, ATIA](https://www.atia.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/ATOB-V14-A9-SheldonErickson.pdf)

### Inferences
- Predictable Chart Writing is effectively a one-slot sentence formula ("I like ___.") used as a class routine. Symbolic Grammar could formalize it: the stem is shown in symbols, and the slot symbol shows which word job goes in the blank. This extends the routine from emergent writers to formula progression.
- "Innovate on a pattern book" fits naturally with formula codes: the fixed frame is the formula, and substitutions are slot fillers. A Brown Bear-style frame for each master formula would give learners a memorable carrier phrase.
- Given the decodable-versus-predictable debate, the curriculum doc should state that predictable patterns are used for *composition and syntax*, not to teach word reading by guessing.

### Gaps
- Repeated-line poems and Mrs. Wishy-Washy were not specifically searched.
- No controlled study of pattern-book *writing* (innovation) outcomes was found. The MSU dissertation's results are unknown.
- No effect-size data was found for Predictable Chart Writing itself. The handouts describe it but did not cite outcome studies in the excerpts.

## 3. Story and paragraph visuals (story spine, story maps, story cubes, hamburger, Four Square, Step Up to Writing, Talk for Writing)

### Takeaway
Story and paragraph structure is taught through a small set of highly predictable visual frames: the Story Spine (sentence-starter chain), Talk for Writing story maps (imitate, innovate, invent), the hamburger paragraph, Four Square, and Step Up to Writing's traffic-light colors. Each maps naturally onto a "paragraph formula" or "story formula" layer above Symbolic Grammar's sentence formulas.

### Cited Findings
- **Story Spine** (Kenn Adams, Synergy Theater): "Once upon a time... Every day... But/Until one day... Because of that... Because of that... Until finally... And ever since then... (And the moral of the story is...)". The "But" versus "Until" wording varies by source. NPR (2026) calls it an 8-sentence tool and traces its spread to Pixar via Rebecca Stockley. Sources give different origin dates (1991 versus the late 1980s). (search summary, not opened). Sources: [NPR via WUNC, 2026-06-23](https://www.wunc.org/2026-06-23/meet-the-creator-of-the-story-spine-an-8-sentence-tool-to-create-and-analyze-stories); [SessionLab](https://www.sessionlab.com/methods/story-spine); [Pulitzer Center classroom activity PDF](https://pulitzercenter.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/Florin%20-%20Story%20Spine%20Activity.pdf)
- **Talk for Writing** (Pie Corbett): Imitation means learning a model text by heart with a story map and actions. Innovation means changing the class map with children's ideas and retelling. Invention means writing a new story on a simple plot pattern (Opening, Build-up, Problem, Resolution, Ending). Corbett frames innovation as "structured play" and invention as "free play". [PROGRAM/PRACTITIONER] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Talk4Writing Inventing Stories PDF](https://www.talk4writing.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/08/Inventing-Stories.pdf); [Talk4Writing EY Innovation PDF](https://www.talk4writing.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/EY-Innovation.pdf); [McGraw-Hill Canada imitation handout](https://www.mheducation.ca/media/wysiwyg/Handout_-_Imitation_Stage.pdf)
- **Rory's Story Cubes**: 9 dice with 54 black-and-white images in total. The publisher's age guidance is 8+. Teachers suggest 1-3 cubes for young writers with a modeled story first, rolling all of them versus adding one image at a time, and BYU's "Statue Stories" lesson turns a cube story into five frozen poses. [VENDOR/PRACTITIONER] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [BYU McKay School lesson](https://education.byu.edu/arts/lessons/statue-stories-with-rory%E2%80%99s-story-cubes); [UBC blog](https://blogs.ubc.ca/samw/?p=153); [K20 Center](https://learn.k20center.ou.edu/tech-tool/668); [Rainbow Resource](https://www.rainbowresource.com/rory-s-story-cubes)
- **Paragraph hamburger**: top bun = topic sentence, fillings = detail sentences, bottom bun = closing sentence. Reading Rockets hosts an Understood.org printable organizer. EdWeek says it is used in "many elementary classrooms across the country". No originator or date was found. [PRACTITIONER] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Reading Rockets paragraph hamburger](https://www.readingrockets.org/classroom/classroom-strategies/paragraph-hamburger); [Understood organizer PDF](https://www.readingrockets.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/Hamburger_Paragraph_Graphic_Organizer_Understood_0.pdf); [EdWeek 2017](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/the-hamburger-method-for-essay-writing/2017/02)
- **Four Square** (Judith S. Gould and Evan Jay Gould, 1999): a four-quadrant planning grid with a central box. It was built for primary grades and is also used in high school. (search summary, not opened, Wikipedia). Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_square_writing_method)
- **Step Up to Writing** (Maureen Auman, Voyager Sopris): green topic sentence ("go"), yellow reasons or details ("slow down"), red explanation or examples ("stop and explain"), green conclusion. One teacher progression aims for one red per yellow by the end of the year. Students highlight the colors in model paragraphs and in their own drafts. [PROGRAM/PRACTITIONER] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Albany (OR) SUTW outline](https://albany.k12.or.us/media/2016/04/Step-Up-to-Writing-Outline.pdf); [Apple Education, Stoplight Paragraph](https://education.apple.com/resource/250013139); [DKG Journal 89-5](https://publication.dkg.org/Journal/89-5/files/basic-html/page11.html)
- **SRSD POW + TREE** (persuasive) and story-grammar mnemonics: POW = Pick ideas, Organize notes, Write. TREE = Topic sentence, Reasons, Explanations, Ending. (search summary, not opened). Source: [Autism CRC executive summary](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/sites/default/files/inline-files/2.010%20Overcoming%20difficulties%20with%20written%20expression%20Executive%20Summary%20FINAL%20WEB.pdf). Research findings are in section 5.

### Inferences
- The Story Spine is itself a predictable "formula" of fixed sentence starters. It could become a story-level formula code, with each spine line carrying an expected sentence formula (for example, "Every day..." pairs with present-habitual or past-habitual tense symbols; "But one day..." pairs with simple past).
- Step Up to Writing shows that color coding at the *paragraph* level is mainstream. Symbolic Grammar needs a paragraph-role key that does not clash with its word-class symbol colors (for example, use borders or icons for paragraph roles and keep fill colors for word jobs).
- Talk for Writing's imitate, innovate, invent sequence matches explicit-first instruction (model, then guided substitution, then independent use) and could frame each genre template unit.
- Story cubes and picture-sequence cards give a low-language way into the content, so students can tell a story while the formula handles the syntax.

### Gaps
- Plot mountain, comic-strip planning, picture-sequence card products and story stones were not specifically searched (tool budget).
- Pinterest writing anchor charts could not be inventoried, since Pinterest did not show up in results.
- No originator was found for the hamburger paragraph.
- Opinion and informational graphic organizers beyond TREE, Four Square and hamburger (for example, OREO) were not searched.

## 5. Research on graphic organizers, visual supports and predictable routines in writing for students with autism or LD

### Takeaway
The evidence is promising but small. Graphic organizers have meta-analytic support for students with disabilities in general (Urton et al. 2024) and single-case support for writing with LD (Boon et al. 2018). For autism specifically, the best writing evidence is SRSD with mnemonic or visual planning (POW + TREE), from multiple-baseline studies. No autism-specific meta-analysis of graphic organizers for *writing* was found.

### Cited Findings
- Urton et al. (2024), a three-level meta-analysis across students with disabilities, found graphic organizers effective for improving academic performance. [RESEARCH] (search summary, not opened). Source: [Taylor & Francis](https://tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09362835.2024.2389080)
- Boon, Barbetta and Paal (2018) synthesized single-case studies of graphic organizers on writing outcomes for students with LD. [RESEARCH] (title and summary only, not opened). Source: [FIU Discovery](https://discovery.fiu.edu/display/pub143081)
- Dexter and Hughes (2011), a meta-analysis on graphic organizers for students with LD, found gains in vocabulary, comprehension and inferential knowledge. This covers reading and content learning, not writing. [RESEARCH] (search summary, not opened). Sources: [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/graphic-organizers-and-students-with-learning-disabilities-a-meta/); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/073194871103400104)
- A 2025 meta-analysis of five single-case studies found moderate-to-strong effects (Tau-U = 0.85) of pictorial or graphic representations on *reading comprehension* for students with ASD. It is a small evidence base. [RESEARCH] (search summary, not opened). Source: [J Autism Dev Disord, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10803-025-07014-4)
- McClure, Pennington and Bewley (2024), a systematic review of experimental writing interventions for students with ASD, notes that relatively little research exists. [RESEARCH] (search summary, not opened; item not linked in results beyond the University of Kentucky scholars page). Source: [UKY Scholars](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/evaluating-the-evidence-base-supporting-writing-instruction-strat/)
- SRSD POW + TREE with six students with high-functioning autism (grades 4 and 6, multiple-baseline, multiple-probe design) improved persuasive writing. One report says 100% of posttest scores exceeded the lowest baseline, a "very effective" rating, and that special education teachers implemented it with fidelity after limited training. Earlier Asaro-Saddler SRSD story-writing work with three children with ASD (grades 2 and 4) suggested benefit. Reviews say SRSD improves writing quality, genre elements and length for students with ASD. [RESEARCH] (search summary, not opened; exact samples unverified). Sources: [Scilit, Teaching Children with HFASD to Write Persuasive Essays](https://www.scilit.net/publications/bfa59331f9da1f8e70ffa6fbfd54e85a); [GMU thesis/repository](https://mars.gmu.edu/items/bb3a6dd6-6533-4ba2-98ff-c81769671f0a/full); [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/480108367.pdf)
- An Autism CRC (Australia, 2018) project compared handwriting, writing-support software and SRSD for autistic students, using video modeling to introduce both the software and SRSD. [RESEARCH REPORT] (search summary, not opened). Source: [Autism CRC executive summary](https://www.autismcrc.com.au/sites/default/files/inline-files/2.010%20Overcoming%20difficulties%20with%20written%20expression%20Executive%20Summary%20FINAL%20WEB.pdf)
- Zajic et al. (2016, *Autism*) note that more research is needed on ASD-specific writing difficulties that non-ASD-specific practices like SRSD do not address. [RESEARCH] (search summary, not opened). Source: [UC Davis PDF](https://b-lab.ucdavis.edu/sites/main/files/file-attachments/writing_paper_1_autism-2016-zajic-1362361316675121.pdf)
- Reading Rockets has articles on using technology (including a writing-process pilot) to support students with ASD. (title only, not opened). Source: [Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/article/using-technology-support-students-autism-spectrum-disorders-writing-process-pilot)

### Inferences
- The research supports pairing Symbolic Grammar's visual formulas with explicit strategy instruction (SRSD-style: model, memorize, guided practice, independent use, self-regulation). The visual alone is not the whole intervention.
- Graphic organizers are a defensible evidence-based layer for paragraph and genre templates. Claims for autism specifically should be framed as "promising, small single-case base".
- Clinical sentence-level systems (Colourful Semantics, Shape Coding) have the most direct link to symbol-based grammar but the thinnest published outcome data. Symbolic Grammar could fill this gap by collecting its own single-case data.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes for Urton et al. (2024) and Boon et al. (2018) were not retrieved.
- No studies were found on predictable *routines* (fixed lesson structure) for writing with autism. Only the routine descriptions (Predictable Chart Writing) were found.
- No research was found on color coding for grammar in ADHD or dyslexia specifically.
