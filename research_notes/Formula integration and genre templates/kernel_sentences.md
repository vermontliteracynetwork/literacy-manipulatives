## 1. Origins and meanings: Harris, Chomsky (1957), school "basic sentence patterns", Hunt's T-unit, O'Hare sentence combining

### Takeaway
"Kernel" began as a technical term (Harris 1957; Chomsky 1957) for the small set of simple, active, declarative sentence types from which all other sentences are derived by transformations; Chomsky dropped it by 1965, but teachers kept it, through sentence combining (Hunt, O'Hare) and school "basic sentence patterns" (Roberts, Kolln), as the name for a bare subject + predicate sentence.

### Cited Findings
- Zellig Harris, "Co-occurrence and Transformation in Linguistic Structure," Language 33 (1957), pp. 283-340, reprinted in Harris 1970: 390-457. (search summary, not opened) — [Zellig Harris, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Zellig_Harris)
- Harris's method reduces a text (especially scientific text) to a sequence of kernel sentences; for English the kernels are a few structures, "chiefly NV and NVPN, NVN and NVNPN, N is N, N is A, N is PN." Transformations are an equivalence relation that partitions the set of sentences. (search summary, not opened) — [Zellig Harris, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Zellig_Harris); [New World Encyclopedia: Zellig Harris](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Zellig_Harris)
- Harris is credited with bringing the term "transformation" into linguistics. (search summary, not opened) — [Zellig Harris, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Zellig_Harris)
- Chomsky's Syntactic Structures (1957) grammar has three components: phrase structure, transformational, and morphophonemic. The passive transformation is the standard example; it depends on phrase-structure rules applying first. (search summary, not opened) — [Britannica: Syntactic Structures](https://britannica.com/topic/Syntactic-Structures); [Syntactic Structures, Wikipedia](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntactic_Structures)
- The book's core claim, as one source summarizes it: a grammar limited to a "kernel" of simple sentences from which all other sentences are built by repeated transformations. In The Logical Structure of Linguistic Theory, kernels were derived by obligatory transformations; kernels were later generalized into a deep structure distinct from surface structure. (search summary, not opened) — [Syntactic Structures, Wikipedia](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntactic_Structures); [Transformational grammar, Wikipedia](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/E-language)
- Chomsky's own definition (section 5.5, "kernel of the language": sentences produced by obligatory transformations only) could not be verified; the PDF host was blocked. — [Chomsky 1957 PDF, UPenn (not reachable)](https://www.ling.upenn.edu/courses/ling5700/Chomsky1957.pdf)
- Aspects of the Theory of Syntax (1965) "quietly abandon[ed] the notion of 'kernel sentence'" and brought in "deep structure," which in a sense replaces it. (search summary, not opened) — [Chronicle, Lingua Franca, 2015](https://www.chronicle.com/blogs/linguafranca/2015/06/21/revolutionary-methodological-preliminaries/)
- Paul Roberts, Patterns of English (Harcourt, Brace, 1956, 314 pp.), written for senior high school English teachers; a 1956 review describes it as presenting a few basic sentence patterns that can be combined and modified in many ways. (search summary, not opened) — [HathiTrust record](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/001902944); [Canadian Journal of Linguistics vol. 2 back matter](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/EB99A39E448488E6E36E9913FD0154A4/S0008413100025007a.pdf/cnj-volume-2-issue-2-cover-and-back-matter.pdf)
- Martha Kolln listed ten basic English sentence patterns that she claims cover the vast majority of English sentences; the claim is limited to statements (not questions, not interjections). (search summary, not opened) — [Tumbug paper, arXiv 2401.09448, citing Kolln & Funk 2006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.09448)
- The current Understanding English Grammar (10th ed.) is by Kolln, Gray and Salvatore; its contents list patterns such as "Pattern I: NP be ADV/TP" through "Pattern X: NP1 V-tr NP2 NP2". (search summary, not opened) — [Pearson listing](https://www.pearson.com/store/en-ca/p/understanding-english-grammar/P200000002253)
- Towson's writing pages group the ten patterns by verb type: be verbs (1-3), linking verbs (4-5), action verbs (6-10). (search summary, not opened) — [Towson OWS: Sentence patterns](https://webapps.towson.edu/ows/sentpatt.htm)
- Kolln's Rhetorical Grammar is reported to use seven basic patterns (a different book, so the count differs). (search summary, not opened) — [UHV: Sentence patterns](https://www.uhv.edu/curriculum-student-achievement/student-success/student-resources/q-z/sentence-patterns)
- Kellogg Hunt coined "T-unit" in 1965: one main clause plus all subordinate clauses attached to it; his study of schoolchildren's writing at three grade levels found T-units grow longer with age. (search summary, not opened) — [T-unit, Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/T-unit)
- Chomsky's kernel ("a basic, no frills SVO sentence") was dropped by syntactic theorists but adopted by teachers for sentence combining. O'Hare (1973) reported that syntactic growth could be had without formal transformational grammar, using cued sentence-combining drills built on kernel sentences; the name "Sentence Combining" left out "transformational." (search summary, not opened) — [Universidad de Chile, Lenguas Modernas article](https://lenguasmodernas.uchile.cl/index.php/LM/article/download/45910/47933/162212); [Yale-New Haven Teachers Institute unit 81.04.06](https://teachersinstitute.yale.edu/curriculum/units/1981/4/81.04.06/2)
- A Montana thesis found sentence-combining practice raised words per T-unit and words per clause (p < .05) but not clauses per T-unit. (search summary, not opened) — [Montana State ScholarWorks thesis](https://scholarworks.montana.edu/bitstreams/ab67093e-0345-4503-951a-bd6f4f4fd57e/download)

### Inferences
- Harris's list of English kernels (NV, NVN, N is A, N is N, N is PN) lines up almost one-to-one with her first code segment: S1 (NV), S2 (NVN), S3 (be + describing word, N is A), S4 (be + naming word, N is N), S5 (be + prepositional phrase, N is PN). Her S6 (feel + describing word) matches the "linking verb" patterns in Kolln's scheme. So her first segment is, in effect, a kernel-pattern label in the Harris/Kolln tradition.
- Her Sn, Q, C, E types correspond to what Chomsky 1957 treated as transformations of kernels (negation, question, imperative), not separate kernels. Her coding treats them as their own sentence types with matching numbers (Q1, Sn3, C2), which is consistent with the transformation idea: Q1 = the S1 kernel turned into a question.
- Kolln's patterns also distinguish two-object and object-complement patterns (Pattern X: NP1 V-tr NP2 NP2) that have no dedicated number on her list yet.

### Gaps
- Chomsky's exact definition and page number of "kernel" were not verified (source blocked).
- The full list of Kolln's ten patterns and Roberts's pattern set were not retrieved.
- O'Hare's 1973 effect sizes and grade (often reported as grade 7) were not confirmed; Hunt's 1965 grade levels and numbers were not confirmed.

## 2. Classroom versions and their evidence: The Writing Revolution, Keys to Literacy, Reading Universe, sentence combining

### Takeaway
In current US practice a "kernel sentence" is a short complete sentence (subject + verb, often no modifiers) that students expand by answering who/what/when/where/why/how, or extend with because/but/so; the direct evidence for these specific programs is thin and mostly program-produced, while the broader evidence for sentence-level instruction rests on sentence-combining research (Graham & Perin 2007, ES 0.50 for grades 4-12; Saddler & Graham 2005).

### Cited Findings
- The Writing Revolution (TWR, Hochman & Wexler): "because, but, so" asks students to finish one stem three ways; because tells why, but shows change of direction, so shows cause and effect. (search summary, not opened; practitioner) — [Shotton Hall Research School reflection](https://researchschool.org.uk/shottonhall/news/reading-reflection-the-writing-revolution); [Ohio DOE Literacy Academy TWR slides](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Learning-in-Ohio/Literacy/Literacy-Academy/2020-Literacy-Academy/3-09-4-09-Advancing-Thinking-Through-Writing-TWR-ACCESSIBLE-PDF.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US)
- A district TWR guide defines a kernel as "a simple, active, declarative sentence containing no modifiers or connectives that may be used in making more elaborate sentences," expanded with who/what/when/where/why/how; it advises starting with kernels that have a clear "who" or "what" and giving the when/where/why prompts. (search summary, not opened; practitioner) — [Baltimore County EWHS TWR sentence expansion](https://sites.google.com/bcps.k12.md.us/ewhs-pl-and-literacy/writing-revolution/sentence-expansion)
- A second-grade guide notes teachers may hold off on "so" depending on students. (search summary, not opened; practitioner) — [TWR reference sheet](https://nathaniel-swain-jygd.squarespace.com/s/TWR-Reference-Sheet-BPPS.pdf)

Headline student findings were not visible in search results. - A Mathematica study (2022-2023) commissioned by TWR reported effects on teacher practice and confidence, not student outcomes. (search summary, not opened; vendor-commissioned) — [TWR research and impact (vendor)](https://www.thewritingrevolution.org/research-impact/)
- TWR's own evidence document opens by noting there has been "so little high-quality research on writing instruction." ### Inferences
- The classroom "kernel" (bare subject + verb, then expand) is essentially her S1.A / S1.PN / S1.P with nothing added; Keys to Literacy's expansion order (subject: article, adjective; predicate: adverb; then phrase) closely matches her code order A then J, then B, then PRP (e.g., S1.A to S1.A.J to S1.A.J.B to S1.A.J.B.PRP, all present on her list).
- Her `(S1.P)C[S1.P]` "because" forms and `(S1.P)C(S1.P.B)` "so" forms are the TWR because/but/so move written as formulas.
- The strongest evidence is for sentence combining (kernel sentences joined into longer ones), mostly grades 2-12; evidence specific to the "kernel then expand with question words" routine for K-1 or for students with significant disabilities was not found.

### Gaps
- No independent RCT of TWR, Keys to Literacy or Reading Universe sentence expansion was found; NORC headline findings could not be read (host blocked).
- No study found on teaching kernels to kindergartners specifically, or to students with intellectual disability or AAC users; Hochman's own "fragments, kernels and expansions" sequence was not retrieved directly.

## 3. Criticisms and limits: outdated linguistics, formulaic writing, age suitability

### Takeaway
The linguistic notion is genuinely outdated in theory (abandoned by 1965), but it survives as a useful teaching label; the main pedagogical criticism (formulaic, constricting writing) is raised mostly in program authors' own interviews and answered by them, with little systematic research either way.

### Cited Findings
- Chomsky later abandoned "kernel"; the Chronicle piece states no linguists today think Aspects presents the right design for grammars. (search summary, not opened) — [Chronicle, Lingua Franca, 2015](https://www.chronicle.com/blogs/linguafranca/2015/06/21/revolutionary-methodological-preliminaries/)
- Kernel was "later dropped by syntactic theorists but adopted by teachers." (search summary, not opened) — [Universidad de Chile, Lenguas Modernas](https://lenguasmodernas.uchile.cl/index.php/LM/article/download/45910/47933/162212)
- Mark Lester argued that teaching transformational grammar does not by itself improve writing (likened to teaching logic and expecting logical behavior). (search summary, not opened) — [Yale-New Haven Teachers Institute unit](https://teachersinstitute.yale.edu/curriculum/units/1981/4/81.04.06/2)
- EdWeek interview: critics say TWR is "too constricting," too focused on process, and stifles creativity; Hochman replies that some may call it formulaic and asks whether a cook bakes a cake without a recipe. (search summary, not opened; program author voice) — [EdWeek Q&A, June 2017](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/q-a-the-writing-revolution-encourages-focus-on-crafting-good-sentences/2017/06)
- Keys to Literacy blog acknowledges the concern that "we don't want to teach children that writing is primarily formulaic," then argues the strategies foster critical thinking. (search summary, not opened; vendor) — [Keys to Literacy: The Writing Revolution](https://keystoliteracy.com/?p=10654)
- Sentence-combining gains can be uneven by measure: words per T-unit and per clause grew but clauses per T-unit did not. (search summary, not opened) — [Montana State thesis](https://scholarworks.montana.edu/bitstreams/ab67093e-0345-4503-951a-bd6f4f4fd57e/download)
- Age: a TWR guide for grade 2 suggests delaying "so"; the Reading Universe demonstration is grade 2; the meta-analytic evidence (Graham & Perin) is grades 4-12. (search summary, not opened) — [TWR reference sheet](https://nathaniel-swain-jygd.squarespace.com/s/TWR-Reference-Sheet-BPPS.pdf); [Reading Universe video](https://readinguniverse.org/resources/video/sentence-writing/sentence-expansion-skill-explainer/using-function-questions-to-write-expanded-sentences); [AdLit: Writing Next](https://www.adlit.org/topics/writing/summary-writing-next)

### Inferences
- "Outdated" applies to kernel as a claim about how the mind or grammar works; it does not undercut using "kernel" as a classroom name for the basic sentence types, which is how Kolln, Keys to Literacy and Reading Universe use the idea.
- Her corpus finding (basic types steady across grades, expansions growing, exact formulas rarely repeating) is consistent with Hunt's T-unit growth pattern: kernels stay, length and attachments grow. That is an argument against letting students stop at exact formulas and for teaching expansion as the growth path.
- The formulaic risk is best managed the way the usage-based research below suggests: vary the words inside a pattern and move to combination and choice, rather than drilling one exact formula.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed critique of kernel-sentence instruction in young children, and no outcome study comparing "exact formula" teaching with "pattern family" teaching, was found.

## 4. How a kernel view can coexist with a specific formula list (families, exemplars vs abstract patterns, usage-based learning)

### Takeaway
Usage-based research supports exactly what her list already does: children start from specific, item-based patterns and generalize gradually; a few repeated, concrete exemplars help a new pattern get noticed, and then varied exemplars help it generalize. Reading each of her codes as "kernel (first segment) + named expansions" turns the list into pattern families without changing it.

### Cited Findings
- Tomasello's verb-island hypothesis: early grammar is organized verb by verb ("hitter," "thing hit"), with no transfer of structure across verbs at first; verb-island constructions later "coalesce into a verb-general transitive construction." Tomasello & Brooks (1998) found children's early transitive and intransitive use was verb-specific. (search summary, not opened) — [Tomasello 2000, Trends in Cognitive Sciences, PDF](https://archive.illc.uva.nl/LaCo/CLAS/clc13/papers/tomasello00tics.pdf); [Lieven 2009, Developing constructions](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/deGruyter/Lieven_Developing_CogLing_2009_1552984.pdf)
- Critiques: Pine et al. (1998) found problems for a strong verb-island account; Yang (2010) argued item-based claims were never tested against a fully productive grammar; Lieven (2009) favors partial generalizations building up early; Goldberg (2006) argues both item-specific and general knowledge must be present. (search summary, not opened) — [Pine et al. 1998 PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_2345703_1/component/file_2345702/Pine_etal_1998.pdf); [Yang 2010 manuscript](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/courses/readings/Yang2010Manu_Zipf.pdf); [Lieven 2009](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/deGruyter/Lieven_Developing_CogLing_2009_1552984.pdf)
- Goldberg and colleagues (Goldberg et al. 2004, 2007; Casenhiser & Goldberg 2005): learning a novel construction was significantly better with skewed input (one exemplar very frequent), and in 2007 when the same exemplars came repeatedly at the start; proposed reason: a shared concrete element makes the abstract pattern easier to spot. (search summary, not opened) — [Pulido 2024, Studies in Second Language Acquisition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/89BEAAAE58A0D8DD11FCEC7D5837BF18/S0272263124000081a.pdf/optimizing_the_input_for_learning_of_l2specific_constructions_the_roles_of_zipfian_and_balanced_input_explicit_rules_and_working_memory.pdf); [Perek & Goldberg 2015, J. Memory and Language](https://adele.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/277/2015/01/15JMLPerek-Goldberg.pdf)
- Later L2 studies found no skewed advantage or a balanced-input advantage; one found low-working-memory learners benefited from reduced variability (skewed) and high-working-memory learners from balanced input. (search summary, not opened) — [Pulido 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/89BEAAAE58A0D8DD11FCEC7D5837BF18/S0272263124000081a.pdf/optimizing_the_input_for_learning_of_l2specific_constructions_the_roles_of_zipfian_and_balanced_input_explicit_rules_and_working_memory.pdf)
- Input variability helps children generalize to new contexts (Wonnacott and colleagues); in a study including children with language-learning disability, high variability (more unique fillers in aXb strings) helped learning the grammatical relation. (search summary, not opened) — [Torkildsen et al., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3973537); [PMC6076332](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6076332)
- Perek & Goldberg (2015): construction learning depends on witnessed usage together with the constructions' functions. (search summary, not opened) — [Perek & Goldberg 2015](https://adele.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/277/2015/01/15JMLPerek-Goldberg.pdf)
- Variation sets (successive utterances with partial repetition, Küntay & Slobin 1996; Waterfall 2006): 20-58% of child-directed utterances repeat words from neighbors; items inside variation sets were produced earlier by children (Waterfall 2006); adults given 20% variation sets segmented words better (Onnis, Waterfall & Edelman 2008); the share of variation sets falls as children age. (search summary, not opened) — [PMC6174075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6174075); [PMC5651173](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5651173)
- Variation sets were more frequent in high-SES caregivers' speech (Hebrew and English; Tal & Arnon 2018); partial repetition was higher in speech to children with ASD than with Down syndrome or typical development (Onnis 2021); one 2021 cross-linguistic study found variation-set estimates often at or below random baselines, which complicates claims of distinctiveness. (search summary, not opened) — [Journal of Child Language: SES effects on variation sets](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/01448A57BBB53B2B5AB02D149752347E); [British Library record, Onnis 2021](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100115380245_0x000001); [PubMed 34953269](https://www.pubmed.gov/34953269/)

### Inferences
- Her code already is "kernel + expansions": the first segment (S1, S2, S3...) names the kernel, and the dotted letters name the expansions. A kernel view needs no new list, only a display layer that groups codes by first segment into families (S1 family: S1.A, S1.A.J, S1.A.J.J, S1.A.J.B, S1.A.J.B.PRP, B,S1.A.J ...). Her 61 S1 entries are already one large family.
- Her choice of four statement formulas for a kindergartner fits the usage-based picture: a few concrete, item-like patterns first (like verb islands), learned through many examples. The skewed-input research supports starting each new pattern with one highly repeated anchor example (e.g., "The frog jumps."), then the variability research supports swapping in many different nouns and verbs so the pattern generalizes, which also answers the "formulaic" worry.
- A teaching sequence that is both kernel-based and formula-based: (1) anchor kernel with repeated example, (2) same kernel with varied words, (3) add one expansion symbol at a time in her code order, (4) show that sentences from different codes share a kernel (family view), (5) combine kernels into her compound/complex forms. Each step is a "variation set" in print: neighboring sentences that differ by one part.
- Because exact formulas rarely repeat in real passages, assessment and reading work should check the kernel and the expansion types present, not an exact code match; the exact code stays useful as a precise description and a writing target.
- Q, C, Sn and E codes sharing numbers with S codes (Q1, C2, Sn3) can be taught as "turn the kernel" moves, the classroom echo of Chomsky's transformations, which keeps the family view consistent.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly compares teaching sentence patterns as families vs as separate formulas in school writing, or exemplar-first vs rule-first sentence instruction for young writers.
- The exemplar-variability studies are lab studies of novel constructions (often adults or L2); their transfer to classroom writing instruction for kindergartners or students with disabilities is an inference, not tested.
